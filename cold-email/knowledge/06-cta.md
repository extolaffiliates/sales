# 06 · CTAs and P.S. lines

## Rules
1. **Exactly one question**, usually the CTA. Ending on two questions is the most common flaw in bad templates (Bullock) [R06].
2. **Ask about interest, not time, in email 1.** Gong (304K emails):

   | CTA type | Meetings booked |
   |---|---|
   | Interest | **30%** |
   | Specific time | 15% |
   | Open-ended | 13% |

   This reverses once a deal is active (specific time 37%) [DATA][R02].
3. **They should be able to reply in 5 words or fewer** (Eric) [SRC].
4. **Foot in the door:** a small yes first raises later compliance [RCT: Guéguen 2002, R06].
5. **Detach** from the outcome. "Neediness is repulsive" (Braun) [R04]. Add a pressure release: "Either way, thanks for reading." (Coleman) or "If not, no worries."
6. **No links or calendar links in email 1.** Once they've said yes, send 2 specific times *or* a calendar link (`12-replies-objections.md`).

## CTA library by type
| Type | Examples | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Interest (default)** | "Worth a look?" · "Open to learning more?" · "Worth exploring in Q1?" · "Opposed to learning more?" · "Is this of interest, or am I way off base?" · "Would an email exchange make sense?" (Braun) · "Curious to learn more?" (Coleman) | Most first touches |
| **Confirmation** | "Is this still the case?" · "Are you already doing X?" · "Just confirm {{example}} is accurate" (Eric) | Problem-sniffing hooks |
| **Offer / resource** | "Want me to send the 3 ideas?" · "Could I send you access?" · "Want to see a 2-minute video of how it'd work for you?" · "Would it be useful if I sent it over?" (Eric, Harvey) | When you have free work to give. **[TEST]** vs interest. Harvey cites +28% for offer CTAs over meeting asks |
| **Risk-minimizer** | "If I could {result} {risk minimizer}, would you be interested in speaking?" (Nick Abraham) | Performance or guarantee offers |
| **"Solve"** | "Is reducing {{pain}} this quarter worth a quick chat?" (Orlob) | Strong pain line above it |
| **No CTA / expert question** | "Have you looked into {{approach}}?" (Braun, "lean back") | **[TEST]**. Senior or skeptical buyers |
| **Redirect** | "Should I reach out to {{full name}} instead given their role?" (Eric) | Email 4 / breakup |

**Avoid:**
- "Are you available Tuesday at 2pm?"
- "Can I get 15 minutes on your calendar?"
- "Let's hop on a call"
- "Would love to schedule…"
- "Let me know if you're interested" (passive, and it stalls even positive replies [R06])
- "Worth a quick chat?" (Harvey calls it overused; use it only after a strong pain line)

## P.S. lines
Many readers read the P.S. first (Zeigarnik effect; Braun, Orlob) [R04]. Use one when the body is very short or when it carries the strongest hook.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Poke the bear | "PS - How do you know SDRs that interview well will perform well?" |
| Future pacing | "PS - How would it feel to run commission statements for 100+ reps in 5 minutes?" |
| A deposit (free value) | "P.S. We work with thousands of {{type}} orgs and can share insights you're free to steal." (Jason Bay) |
| Testimonial | "PS - Here's what changed after Albert at Gong switched." |
| Remove friction | "P.S. That free test is really free and we can launch it in under 15 minutes." (Eric) |
| Case-study overwhelm | "P.S. Here's some of our 2024 work: …" (Eric) |
| Opt-out / compliance | "P.S. If this isn't relevant, let me know and I'll stop." (Martignoni) |

One campaign doubled meetings from 10% to 20% by adding a custom P.S. on top of a custom first line (Tuwiner) [SELF-REPORTED][R04].
