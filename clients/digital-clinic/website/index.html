The problem

Why good start-ups stall

Most start-ups that stall have a product that works. What they are missing is someone experienced in the room who has no day job in the business and can ask the awkward questions early.

Building before anyone has agreed to pay

Months go into the product. The first serious conversation about price happens after launch.

Selling to the wrong person

The people who love the product are often not the people who hold the budget.

No proof a buyer trusts

A demo and a few friendly users are not evidence. Buyers and investors want results they can check.

Governance left too late

Contracts, data protection, regulation and IP get dealt with when a customer asks, and the deal waits while they are fixed.

A founder deciding alone

With no independent challenge, the plan is only tested when the money starts to run out.

Method

How the clinic works

The same four steps for every company, whatever its sector or stage. You always see us first, and we stay responsible for the plan.

  1. 1Examine

    Consultation

    A discovery session with our founder. We look at the product, the customers, the money and the team.

  2. 2Diagnose

    Written diagnosis

    A short report on where the company is, what is missing, and the order to fix it in.

  3. 3Prescribe

    Referral to specialists

    We send you to the departments you need, with our notes, so no specialist starts from scratch.

  4. 4Monitor

    Follow-up

    Regular reviews against the plan. For many companies this becomes an adviser or board relationship.

The pathway

Five stages from idea to commercialisation

Your diagnosis places the company on this pathway. It shows what has been done, what has been skipped, and what the next customer or investor will ask for.

  1. Stage 1

    Ideation

    Shape the idea with the people who will use it and the people who will pay for it.

  2. Stage 2

    Proof of principle

    Test the riskiest assumption with a cheap prototype that is allowed to fail fast.

  3. Stage 3

    Ready to sell

    Put in place what your market requires before it can buy: compliance, contracts, data protection, IP.

  4. Stage 4

    First customers

    Run pilots with agreed measures, so each one produces evidence and a reference.

  5. Stage 5

    Commercialisation

    Turn pilots into contracts. Set pricing, a repeatable sales process and recurring revenue.

And after that. Commercialisation is where a lot of advisers leave. A non-executive stays. Once the company is trading we remain on the board or alongside it, for growth, funding rounds and the decisions that get harder as the company gets bigger.

How we work
