# 05 · Body: problem, bridge and proof

## Length and readability
- **50–90 words.** Extend to 125 only when AI-researched ideas or detail justify it (Eric) [SRC]. Email 1 under 75 is better (Harvey) [SRC].
- The data:
  - 49 words vs 127: **4.8% vs 2.7% replies** [RCT][R06]
  - Gong: under 100 words, 3–4 sentences [R02]
  - Lavender: 0–50 words +23% [R02]
- **Reading grade 3–7** (Lavender 1.66x; Boomerang). Plain words make you seem *smarter* (Oppenheimer) [R03].
- **Sentences under 20 words; paragraphs of 1–2 lines; white space.** Most cold email is read on a phone [R02].
- **The "them : us" ratio is at least 3:1 in sentences** (Eric) [SRC]. "You" should outnumber "I/we".

## Structures (pick one; all lead to problem → proof → ask)

|---|---|---|

| **4T** | Truth → Think question → Third-party credibility → Talk (yes/no or a free taste) | Braun [R04] |
| **Safe Space** | Observation → Problem → "we see this with others, you're not alone" → Solution → CTA | Allred [R04] |
| **Orlob 4-part** | Relevant intro → agitate (the dilemma) → future state, as an outcome not features → "Solve" yes/no CTA | Orlob [R04] |
| **Before → After** | The customer's "before" pain in their own words → the "after" → "Think this might help?" | Braun [R04] |
| **Mouse Trap** | Trigger → value-prop question (1–2 sentences) | Allred [R04] |

## The problem line
- **Name the pain better than they could** (Orlob) [R04]. Use **their words**, mined from case studies and reviews (Braun: "good cold email copy isn't written, it's found") [R04].
- **Use a dilemma** for vividness: "You either hit headcount and sacrifice quality, or hire strong reps and miss your number." [R04]
- **Frame it as a loss** (Kahneman) [R03]. Use **emotional language** sparingly: "sipping peanut butter through a straw" (Braun) [R04].
- **Use neutral, non-accusatory wording:** "not sure if this is on your radar…" Hedged hypotheses ("I imagine", "I suspect") lower resistance [R04].
- **One problem per email** (Jed) [R04]. The next problem goes in the next email.

## Proof
- **Precise and matched to them:** "Helping someone increase their ROAS by 3.72x is much more believable than by 4x" (Eric) [R04].
- Use a peer of a similar size or industry (Berman, Cegelski) [R02][R04]. A huge-brand case study can signal "expensive" to small companies, so test with and without the name [R04].
- **Formula:** specific client + specific result + specific timeframe (Berman) [R02].
- **Credibility multiplies personalization** (Joinson & Reips: a personal salutation only worked from a high-status sender) [RCT][R06].
- **No proof yet?** Use a risk reversal or free work (`02-offers.md`). **Never invent proof.** Only `libraries/proof-bank.md` is allowed.

## Cut it down (Eric's three passes) [SRC]
1. **Delete fluff (−20%):** greetings, "I wanted to", hedges like "perhaps".
2. **Compress (−15%):** "We built a platform that can do X" → "We do X".
3. **Cut adjectives (−10%):** keep only data. Delete "great", "powerful", "robust".

**Example:** 94 → 52 words (Eric) [R04]:
> Sarah, saw you sell to sales leaders. / Noticed John is a BDR on the team. Have you figured out how he could leverage better data and GPT-4 for prospecting, or still manual? / Most teams save 10 min per prospect and 3x volume without adding headcount. / Worth a look?

## Banned in the body
- **Feature dumps; bullets** (except the creative-ideas format); bold, italics or links in email 1.
- **Buzzwords:** leverage, synergy, solution, platform, cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionize, streamline, seamless, robust, innovative, "AI-powered", ROI without a number.
- **Informative "about us" tone** (−26% replies, Lavender) [R04].
- **Em dashes** (an AI tell; Eric's hard rule #1, Harvey) [SRC].
- "Picture this", "Here's what I keep seeing", before/after lists, "No pressure".
- **Proactive objection-handling paragraphs.** Space is precious (Cegelski) [R04].
