# 09 · Sequences and follow-ups

## The default: 4 emails as two threaded pairs (Eric, GEX Wrapped 2024) [R04]
| Step | Day | Thread | Job | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | **New** subject | Best signal, best proof, clearest offer | Full first-touch rules, 50–90 words |
| 2 | 3–4 | **Reply in thread 1** (blank subject) | Depth: how the case study happened, *or* a short new micro-reason | 15–60 words. "Mouse Trap", stats hook, or creative ideas |
| 3 | 7–8 | **New** subject | A **different offer type** (time ↔ money) and a lower-friction CTA ("if I made a video of our process for you, would you watch it?") | Must stand completely alone. Consider dropping AI personalization |
| 4 | 11–12 | **Reply in thread 3** | The "right person" redirect or a resource offer. Signals the end | "{{first_name}} – I know there's about {{employee_count}} employees at {{company}} and perhaps {{job}} isn't your responsibility. Should I reach out to {{full name}} instead?" |

**Why two threads:** "following up with everything in one thread… just reminds the prospect they didn't respond." Email 1 gets the most positive replies, so a fresh thread at email 3 gets "out of the blue" attention [R04].

## Rules
- **Every follow-up adds something new:** a new angle, a different problem or offer type, new proof, a resource, a screenshot, or a referral ask.
- **Never open with:**
  - "Following up on my last note"
  - "One more thought"
  - "{{first_name}}, quick one"
  - "Just checking in", "Bubbling this up", "Making sure you saw", "Circling back"
  - any reference to earlier emails

  (Eric, Coleman, Ingram) [R04][SRC]
- **Short is good; empty is bad.** A 1–2 line follow-up with a new micro-reason can nearly double replies vs re-pitching (Harvey; Sam Nelson's bumps). A content-free bump only reminds them why they ignored you [R04].
- **Rotate offer types** across steps (save time → make money → save money) [SRC].
- **If email 3 beats email 1, promote it to email 1** [R04].
- **Images:** none in email 1. Test one relevant screenshot in email 2 or 3 (30MPC: +17% replies) [R04].

## Length by market
| Situation | Sequence |
|---|---|
| **Huge market (80K+) and a yes/no offer** (selling a business, taxes) | **2 emails**, then move on. "Follow up is key in sales, but not in outbound marketing" (Eric) [R04] |
| **Standard B2B (volume)** | **4 emails** over about 12 days (default) |
| **Tier-1 / high-value accounts** | 6–7 emails plus LinkedIn and calls over about 30 days. Florin: 40%+ of replies come after step 8 for SDR teams. Becc: email 5 converts best [R02][R04] |

Data:
- Smartlead: 1 email ≈ 12 replies per 1K contacts, 3 emails ≈ 25, 7 emails ≈ 40, with diminishing returns.
- Hunter: with follow-ups 4.9% vs without 3.0% [R04].

## Reusing your TAM (Eric) [R04]
- Re-contact the same list **every 3 months** (2 months at minimum) with **fresh data and new messaging**. "No one actually remembers a cold email."
- **Volume maths:**
  - leads needed per month ÷ positive replies per contact = contacts per month
  - Example: 10 meetings at 1/250 means about 2,500 contacts a month.
  - If the TAM is small, spread the sends so you don't burn through it.

## Breakups
- **Use:** the right-person redirect with an opt-out P.S. (S22, S23), "bye for now" (Allred, 12–15% claimed), or Florin's one-liners ("happy to put this on the back-burner. Let me know either way").
- **Never:** guilt, sarcasm, "this is my last attempt…", or a breakup that keeps pitching (Harvey's bad example) [R04][SRC].

## Multichannel (tier 1) [R05]
- **LinkedIn:** connect plus an engagement step. LinkedIn replies about 10% vs 3.4% for email (vendor data). Sam Nelson reused the email's two custom lines in InMails and call openers [R04].
- **Personalized Loom/video at step 2:** 10–16% replies reported. It must be genuinely specific; never fake a custom video [R05].
- **Calls:** dial-to-meeting conversion is 2–3%. Open the call with the email's first two lines (Sam Nelson) [R04].
