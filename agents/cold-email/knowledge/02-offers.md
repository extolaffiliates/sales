# 02 · Offers

"Nothing is more important in your cold outreach than putting an offer in front of someone that is a no brainer." (Nowoslawski [R04]). The ready-made templates are in `libraries/offer-library.md`.

## The "why wouldn't they reply?" check (Nowoslawski) [R02][R04]
List the hidden objections, then remove each one *inside* the offer:
- How long will it take?
- Is it really better?
- Who else got this result, and are they like me?
- What if it fails? ("If this works I get a pat on the back; if not, I get fired.")
- We already have someone.

## Offer types
- **Every offer is one of: save time / make money / save money** (B2B; reduce risk is a fourth) [R04][SRC]. Rotate them across variants and follow-ups.
- **Saraev [R01]:** "I'll save you time" is weak. Sell a money outcome with proof, or reduce the risk.
- **Hormozi's value equation [R06]:** (dream outcome × perceived likelihood) ÷ (time delay × effort).

## The offer ladder for email 1 (lower = easier to say yes)
1. **Free work they simply receive.** Fixed-slot "3 ideas"; a 2–3 minute Loom teardown of their site; a list you built for them; a benchmark.
   - Lieben: "Think about the first thing you would do for them if you signed them… And do it already, for free." [R04]
   - Saraev: build the automation first, then show it in a Loom [R06].
2. **A free test or trial with a defined result.** GEX free campaign: "We launch the campaign for you and give any leads generated straight to your inbox." [R04]
3. **Pay per result.** "If I could {result} {risk minimizer} would you be interested in speaking?" (Nick Abraham) [R04]
4. **Guarantees.** Saraev: a $250 gift card if we don't deliver [R01]; Forge Partnerships: "we'll work free until we hit it" [R06].
   - Eric *refuses* guarantees, because results depend on the market. He uses an "anti-guarantee" plus a free test instead [R04]. **[TEST]**
5. **A meeting.** Never the email-1 ask for cold traffic.

## Making the offer strong
- **Define the result** ("a meeting held with a decision maker at a company with 10+ staff"). Buyers distrust vague performance offers [R06].
- **Name a number and a timeframe.** Timeline hooks ("10 booked calls in 30 days") reportedly get 2.3x the replies of problem hooks **[TEST]** [R06].
- **Chunk up** (bigger outcomes: "no need to hire SDRs") **or down** (small jobs: "we manage your deliverability") when the core pitch is saturated [R04].
- **Reframe the assumption.** "If you need an accountant, we can help" becomes "How do you know your current accountant is using every legal loophole?" [R04]
- **A straight line to cash.** The freebie must lead to the paid offer. GEX's free lists got replies but didn't convert to meetings [R04].
- **Free material must be genuinely good.** Hormozi: "the market judges your free material." A DMARC audit offer flopped because nobody felt the pain [R04].
- **Test before building.** "If I did [the lead magnet], would you be the right person to share it with?" (Eric) [R04].

## Problem awareness
Prospects have to *feel* the problem. If they don't know they have it, use a **reveal-the-problem** magnet:
- record their missed call
- show their AI-search ranking
- run a speed-to-lead test

Don't use an audit offer about a problem they don't know they have [R04][R06].

## Defaults for Extol (AI automation agency) [R06]
- **Niches with clear pain:**
  - home services (missed calls)
  - dental / med spa
  - real estate (speed to lead)
  - legal and finance (high retainers)
  - underserved picks: vet, logistics, property management
- **Best first offers:** the missed-call recording, database reactivation (pay per appointment), a Loom teardown, and fixed-slot "3 automations" ideas.
