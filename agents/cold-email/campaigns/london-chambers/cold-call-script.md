# Cold Call Script – Extol Affiliates, London Chambers of Commerce

**AI automation · Christmas members' offer · Nov–Dec 2026**

Built on the structure and tone of the *Old Client Review* script (via the Tavistock Protect v2 script): opener by lead type, purpose that removes the fear of being sold to, one diagnostic question, a clear and limited next step, an objection table, close the loop, follow-ups.

Call list: [London Chambers of Commerces](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1x19O_yJF3VEgYmYpjOiY34cY25KocvsNwoH_hDCs82M/edit) (one tab per chamber).

---

## Before you dial

**Fill these in once, before day 1.** Every `[ ]` in this script is one of these.

| Field | Value | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Caller | [Your name], [role], Extol Affiliates | Say your real name and role. |
| Chamber(s) we have actually joined | [e.g. Merton] | Only say "we've just joined" for these. Every other chamber uses **Opener B**. |
| Members' price | £[price] for one automation, built and running | Our suggestion is in Section 11. Don't say "normally £X" unless we have really charged £X before (CMA pricing rules). |
| Running cost after build | £[0 / £Y a month] | Know this before you call. "How much?" always comes. |
| Members' price runs until | **Sunday 31 January 2027** (builds agreed by then) | One rule everywhere. Owners compare notes within a chamber. |
| December build slots | [N], first come, first served | Real capacity is the only scarcity we use. Last calls go out **Friday 18 December** (25 Dec is a Friday). |
| Build time | Live within [n] days | Know the real number. "Before Christmas" is too late for hospitality. |
| Testimonial in return | Yes: a short review and permission to describe the result | This is how we fill `libraries/proof-bank.md`. |

**Pick the opener by lead type.**

- Same chamber we've joined → **Opener A** (new member)
- A London chamber we haven't joined → **Opener B** (new to the London chambers)
- Newham Business Awards tab: winners, finalists and sponsors → **Opener C** (congratulations)
- The number reaches reception or a general line → **Opener D** (gatekeeper)

**Read the row first (30 seconds).** Look at *Company Name, Primary Category, Description, Services Offered, Business Hours* and, on the Hillingdon tab, *Member Offers*. Pick **one** detail to mention early, for example "I saw you do 24/7 call-outs" or "I saw you run a members' discount". Then find their industry in the **cheat sheet (Section 10)** so you know which automation to suggest.

**Skip or re-route some rows.**

- **Skip:** councils and public bodies, universities, the chamber itself, and branches of national chains (for example a mobile phone store). Their decisions are made at head office.
- **Re-route:** AI, IT, web, marketing and digital agencies are likely partners, not buyers. Use the partner line in Section 10.
- **No phone number:** email them instead. Don't guess a number.

**Check TPS and CTPS (PECR).** Before dialling, screen every number against TPS (sole traders and partnerships) and CTPS (companies). Don't call a registered number unless that business has told us it's happy to hear from us. Also check each chamber's directory terms: most allow member-to-member contact but frown on hard selling, so this script gets to know people first.

**Recording.** If calls are recorded, say so (the line is in each opener). If they're not, leave the line out.

**Proof.** We have no case studies yet (`libraries/proof-bank.md` is empty). **Never name a client, a number or a result we don't have.** The honest line, "we're new and building local case studies", is part of the offer, not a weakness.

## 1. Opener

### Opener A: fellow member (a chamber we've joined)

"Hi [First name], it's [Your name] from Extol Affiliates. We've just joined [Chamber], and rather than wait to bump into everyone at the next event, I'm ringing round members to get to know who's who. Do you have a quick minute?"

**Yes** → continue

**No** → "No problem at all. Your listing says you're open [business hours]. Is there a quieter time I could try you?"

"Just to let you know, this call is recorded for quality." *(only if recorded)*

Then one detail from the row:

"I had a look at your listing. You [do X / have been with the chamber a while / run a member offer], so you probably know the chamber better than I do."

### Opener B: a London chamber we haven't joined yet

"Hi [First name], it's [Your name] from Extol Affiliates. We're a small London business that's new to the chamber scene, and I'm getting to know members across the London chambers. [Company] came up on the [Chamber] directory. Do you have a quick minute?"

**Yes** → continue

**No** → "No problem at all. When would suit you better?"

***Coaching note:*** *never say or imply we're a member of their chamber. If they ask, say "Not yet, we're with [Chamber we joined]. I'm getting to know people across London first."*

### Opener C: Newham Business Awards (winners, finalists, sponsors)

"Hi [First name], it's [Your name] from Extol Affiliates. I saw [Company] was [winner / finalist / a sponsor] at the Newham Business Awards [year] for [category]. Congratulations. We're new to the chamber and I'm getting to know members. Do you have a quick minute?"

**Yes** → continue

**No** → "No problem at all. When would suit you better?"

***Coaching note:*** *check the year and category on the Newham Awards tab before you say them. Getting it wrong is worse than not mentioning it.*

### Opener D: gatekeeper or general line

"Hi, it's [Your name] from Extol Affiliates. We're [new members of / new to] [Chamber], and I'm hoping to speak to whoever runs [Company] day to day. Who would that be?"

Then: "Could you put me through? It's a member-to-member call from the chamber directory."

**If they ask what it's about:** "We're getting to know local members, and we do automation for small businesses. It's a two-minute call."

**If they're not available:** "When's the best time to catch [Name]?" Write the name and time in the sheet.

## 2. Purpose: disarm the sales fear

"The reason for the call is simple. We're new, and I'd like to know what you do, so that if I meet someone who needs you, I can send them your way."

"I'll be honest with you: we do AI and automation for small businesses, so I'll mention one thing at the end. But this isn't a hard sell. I'd rather hear about you first."

*Pause.*

"So what does [Company] do, and who are your best customers?"

*Listen. Write down their words. Don't pitch yet.*

***Coaching note:*** *the referral offer has to be real. If you promise to pass work on, write their ideal customer in the sheet and actually do it. It's how chambers work, and it's what makes them take your call next time.*

---

## 3. Diagnostic question: find the one repeated job

Ask **one** of these. Don't stack them. Use the cheat sheet (Section 10) to pick the one for their industry.

### Option A: missed enquiries

- "When someone rings or messages while you're busy, or after hours, what happens to it?"
- "Roughly how many of those do you think go unanswered in a week?"

### Option B: admin time

- "What's the one admin job you or your team do every week that you'd happily never do again?"
- "Who does it now, and how long does it take them?"

### Option C: follow-up

- "When someone gets a quote from you and goes quiet, does anyone chase them?"
- "And once a job's done, do you ask every customer for a review?"

### Option D: the Christmas question

- "Is December your busiest month or your quietest?"
  - **Busy** → "So what tends to slip when it gets mad?"
  - **Quiet** → "So is January a fresh start? What would you want running by then?"

***Coaching note:*** *write down the exact words they use for the problem ("enquiries pile up in DMs", "chasing paperwork"). You'll say them back in Section 5 and in the follow-up. Their words sell better than ours.*

---

## 4. Handle "We're fine / we don't need AI"

**Acknowledge → Reframe → Normalise.**

"That's fair, and lots of people say the same. It isn't really about AI. It's about whether there's one job that takes hours every week and could run by itself."

Then:

"Nearly every small business has one: answering the same five questions, chasing paperwork, or booking appointments by hand. That's the only thing we'd look at."

No arguing. No pressure. If they still say no, go straight to Section 8 and ask about their ideal customer.

---

## 5. The members' offer: clear and finite

"Here's what we're doing for chamber members before New Year. We pick **one** job in your business, [the thing they said in Section 3], and build the automation for it, **for £[price], up and running within [n] days.**

- built around how you already work, with your tone and your answers
- you see it working before it goes live
- no contract and no monthly fee [for the first N months / unless you want us to look after it]

If it doesn't save you time, you've lost very little, and you can switch it off."

**Why so low? Say it before they ask:**

"Two honest reasons. We're new to the chamber and we want local businesses who can vouch for us, so all we ask in return is a short review if you're happy with it. And it's Christmas: we'd rather spend December building than waiting for January."

***Coaching note:*** *the "two honest reasons" line matters. A low price with no reason sounds like low quality. A low price with a real reason sounds like a good deal.*

---

## 6. Next step: a 15-minute chat, not a sale

"The easiest next step is 15 minutes. I'll show you two or three things we could automate for [Company], and you pick one, or none."

"I'm local, so I can pop in for a coffee, or we can do a video call if that's easier. Are mornings or afternoons usually quieter for you?"

Then:

"Is there anyone else who'd want to see it, for example whoever handles [the bookings / the admin]?"

### If they hesitate: the two outcomes

"It's 15 minutes, and there are only two ways it goes:

1. You spot one thing worth automating, and it's running within [n] days at the members' price.
2. You don't, and you've met another member who'll send work your way where I can.

Either way, the coffee's on me."

## 7. Common objections

| They say | What it means | You say |
|---|---|---|
| "Is this a sales call?" | I don't trust you yet. | "Fair question. Partly, yes: there's one offer I'll mention. But mainly I'm getting to know members. If it's not for you, no problem at all." |
| "We don't need AI." / "Not for us." | AI sounds expensive, risky or irrelevant. | "Totally fair. Forget the word AI. Is there one job that eats an hour a day? That's all we'd look at." |
| "It's Christmas, we're flat out." | I don't have time to think about this. | "Completely understand, and that's why we do the work, not you. Shall we book the 15 minutes for the first week of January? The members' price still stands then." |
| "Just send me an email." | Get off the phone politely. | "Happy to. So it's not a generic one: was it the [Option A / B] one that sounded most useful? I'll send just that." Get their email, then: "And shall I pencil in 15 minutes so it doesn't get lost?" |
| "How much is it?" | Is this going to cost a fortune? | "£[price] for one automation, built and running. The software behind it is usually £[Y] a month, and I'll tell you the exact number before you agree to anything." |
| "We tried a chatbot. It was rubbish." | I've been burned before. | "That's really common. Most are generic. We build it around your questions and your tone, and you test it before any customer sees it." |
| "What about our data / GDPR?" | I don't want to get into trouble. | "Good question. It runs inside your own accounts, and we'll sign a data processing agreement. We don't put sensitive data into AI tools without that in place." *(Confirm our DPA and tools before saying this.)* |
| "We've got an IT person / agency." | We're covered. | "Great, we're happy to work with them. This is the day-to-day workflow side, not the IT itself." |
| "I'm not the right person." | Wrong contact. | "Who would be? Would you mind if I said you suggested I call?" |
| "Call me in the new year." | Not a priority yet. | "Of course. OK if I ring you on [Tuesday 5 January]? The members' price runs to the end of January." Log the date in the sheet. |
| "Are you even a member?" (Opener B) | Are you being straight with me? | "Not of [their chamber] yet. We've just joined [our chamber], and I'm getting to know people across London first." |
| "How do I know you're any good?" | Where's your proof? | "Honest answer: we're new to the chamber, so I'm not going to quote clients you can't check. That's why the price is low and you see it working before it goes live." |

## 7b. Voicemail (20 seconds, no pitch)

"Hi [First name], it's [Your name] from Extol Affiliates. We've just joined [Chamber] and I'm ringing round to get to know members. Nothing urgent. I'll try you again on [day], or you can reach me on [number]. Thanks, bye."

***Coaching note:*** *never pitch on a voicemail. Its only job is to make the second call a warm one. Try each row up to three times, on different days and at different times of day.*

---

## 8. Close the loop

"Great, I'll book you in for [Day / Time] and send a calendar invite. What's the best email for you?"

Then confirm the address for a coffee visit, or send a video link.

**Before you hang up, always ask (even if they said no):**

"Last thing: who's your ideal customer? If I meet one, I'll send them your way."

**After the call, update the sheet.** Add these columns to each chamber tab if they're not there yet:

`call_outcome` · `called_on` · `called_by` · `decision_maker` · `pain_in_their_words` · `automation_idea` · `next_step_date` · `whatsapp_ok` · `their_ideal_customer`

`call_outcome` values: *booked · callback · send-info · not-now (date) · not-interested · wrong-person · voicemail · skip*

**Mindset reminder (read before every call)**

*You are not selling AI. You're a new neighbour finding the one job that steals their week.*

## 10. Industry cheat sheet: what to offer whom

Categories are taken from the chamber tabs. The most common are professional and business services, property and construction, retail, hospitality, health and beauty, and charities.

| Industry (as listed) | Ask this (Section 3) | Automation to offer | Christmas / New Year angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Restaurants, pubs, bars, catering, hotels** | "What happens to booking calls and DMs during service?" | WhatsApp, Instagram and phone booking assistant that answers menu and date questions and takes deposits; automatic review requests after the visit | Christmas party enquiries peak right now |
| **Retail (food and non-food), jewellers** | "How many 'are you open / do you have it in stock' messages do you answer?" | FAQ and stock-question assistant on WhatsApp, Instagram and the website; post-purchase thank-you and review message | Gift season now; a January sale list from this year's buyers |
| **Health and beauty, wellbeing, fitness, personal trainers, therapy** | "What do no-shows and last-minute cancellations cost you?" | Reminders, automatic rebooking nudges, missed-call text-back, waitlist fill | The New Year resolution rush in January |
| **Estate and lettings agents, property, surveyors** | "How fast does a new portal enquiry get a reply?" | Instant enquiry reply plus viewing booking; tenant maintenance request triage | "Is January busy for enquiries?" Let them say it. |
| **Building services, trades, plumbing, electrical, pest control, construction** | "What happens to calls while you're on a job?" | Missed-call text-back, quote follow-up, job booking and reminders | Winter emergency call-outs (boilers, leaks) |
| **Accountants, bookkeepers, financial services, insurance brokers** | "How much time goes on chasing clients for paperwork?" | Document-chasing reminders, client onboarding forms, inbox triage | **Self Assessment deadline, 31 January.** The strongest angle in the list. |
| **Solicitors and legal** | "How are new enquiries handled before a fee earner sees them?" | Intake form plus enquiry triage, ID and document chasing, appointment booking | January is a busy time for family and property work. Ask, don't assert. |
| **Business services, consultants, coaches, HR, training** | "What happens after a sales call: notes, proposal, follow-up?" | Call notes into the CRM automatically, proposal follow-up, content repurposing | "Set up your 2027 pipeline before January" |
| **Recruitment** | "How long does a first CV sift take?" | CV shortlist summaries, candidate status updates, interview scheduling | January job-hunting surge |
| **Care homes, health and social care** | "How are family enquiries and tour requests handled?" | Enquiry reply and tour booking; shift-fill messages to bank staff | Winter staffing pressure. **Keep health data out of AI tools without a DPA.** |
| **Transport, storage, logistics, wholesale** | "How many 'where's my order' calls do you get?" | Tracking update messages, quote request auto-reply, invoice reminders | Peak season deliveries |
| **Engineering and manufacturing** | "How do quote requests come in, and who types them up?" | Email-to-quote and email-to-order entry, spec-sheet answers | Year-end order rush |
| **Charities, CICs, social enterprises** | "How do donors get thanked, and how long does it take?" | Automatic thank-yous, Gift Aid declaration chasing, volunteer rota messages | Christmas appeal season. Consider a lower charity price. |
| **Education and training** | "What happens to course enquiries outside office hours?" | Enquiry assistant, enrolment follow-up, reminders | January term starts |
| **AI, IT, web, marketing, digital, print, creative** | *Partner line:* "Do your clients ever ask you for AI or automation?" | A referral swap or white-label build partnership, not a sale | "Add an AI offer for your clients in the new year" |

## 11. Strategy notes (for whoever runs the campaign)

**Suggested price.** One automation for a fixed £[price]. Our suggestion is **£197–£297 one-off**, enough to cover build time and filter out people who aren't serious, small enough that an owner can say yes without a second opinion. Free gets more yeses but fewer finished projects and weaker testimonials. Name a real running cost up front.

**What the offer is really buying for us** (the reason a low price is rational):

1. **Proof.** The proof bank is empty. Five finished chamber projects with reviews are worth more than five full-price ones.
2. **Foot in the door.** A small first purchase makes a second, larger one (monthly care, more automations) far easier.
3. **Chamber referrals.** One happy member gets mentioned at the next breakfast.

**Timing.**

- **Now to 18 Dec:** call. Owners are reachable and the Christmas angle is fresh.
- **21 Dec to 1 Jan:** don't call. Send only promised follow-ups.
- **From 4 Jan:** call back every "not now" on the date they gave.

Hospitality and retail are slammed in December, so book them for January. Accountants are busy in January, so book them **now**, before Self Assessment season.

**Order to dial.** Start with the chamber we joined (warmest), then the Newham Awards tab, then the other chambers. Within each, start with the industries with the strongest Christmas angle: accountants, hospitality, trades, health and beauty.

**Measure from day 1.** Record dials → conversations → meetings → paid builds, by opener and by industry. After about 50 conversations, keep the opener and diagnostic question that book the most meetings, and drop the rest.

**Risks to watch.**

- **Chamber goodwill.** One pushy call gets talked about. That's why the script asks about them first and always offers a referral.
- **Delivery.** Don't book more builds than we can finish by early January. A late Christmas offer hurts more than no offer.
- **Price anchoring.** Present the price as a chamber members' price until 31 January, not "cheap". That keeps the normal price credible later in 2027.
