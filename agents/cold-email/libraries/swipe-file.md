## 1. Observation → Problem → Proof → Interest CTA (the default)

```
Subject: 4 new sdrs

Hi Dana,

Saw Acme is hiring 4 SDRs. Usually that means the pipeline target just went up.

The hard part is the first 90 days. New reps burn good leads while they ramp.

We helped Northwind get new SDRs to their first meeting in 9 days instead of 40.

Worth a look for your new hires?

Sam
```
**Why it works:**
- The trigger is real and checkable, and "If A, then B is probably true" turns it into a problem (Braun).
- It frames the problem as a loss.
- The proof is one line with a matched peer and a timeframe (Berman).
- The CTA asks about interest (Gong: 30% vs 15% for a time ask).
- 54 words.

## 2. Icebreaker → Pitch → Proof → CTA (Saraev, with deep personalization)

```
Subject: leeds dispatch

Hi Tom,

Noticed you're hiring a second dispatcher and your site now covers Bradford too. Guessing the phones are busier than the team right now.

We set up an AI receptionist that books jobs straight into your calendar, even after hours.

A 9-van plumber in Sheffield went from missing 30% of calls to under 5% in a month.

Want me to send a 2-minute video of how it'd work for you?
```
**Why it works:**
- The opener stacks two verified facts from a website scrape (Saraev's multi-line icebreaker).
- The pitch is one plain-English outcome with no "AI-powered".
- The CTA is an offer (a Loom), which is the **[TEST]** variant to run against an interest CTA.

## 3. Logistics → Background → Case study → Risk reversal (Saraev's older 4-step)

```
Subject: your google reviews

Hi Priya,

Found Bright Smile while looking at dental clinics in Manchester with great reviews but no online booking.

My team builds booking and reminder systems for clinics like yours.

A clinic in Salford dropped no-shows from 18% to 6% in two months. If yours don't drop within 60 days, you don't pay.

Open to seeing how?
```
**Why it works:** each part has one job:
1. **Logistics:** where and why we found them.
2. **Background:** who we are, in one line.
3. **Case study:** a specific result.
4. **Risk reversal:** the guarantee answers "what if it doesn't work?" before they can ask it.

## 4. 4T: Truth → Think → Third-party → Talk (Josh Braun)

```
Subject: renewals

Hi Marcus,

Noticed your team moved to annual contracts this year.

Not sure if this is on your radar. When renewals all land in one quarter, churn tends to hide until it's too late to save the account.

A few CS teams like Loopwise now flag at-risk accounts 90 days out.

No rush, but is this worth a conversation?
```
**Why it works:**
- It opens on a factual truth, then asks a neutral "think" question that doesn't accuse.
- The credibility is light.
- Braun's detached CTA makes saying no easy.

## 5. Diagnostic question (Jed Mahrle style, no signal needed)

```
Subject: lead response time

Hi Alex,

When a lead fills in your quote form at 9pm, how long until someone calls them back?

For most roofing companies it's the next morning. By then about half have already booked whoever answered first.

We set up instant text-back and call-back for roofers, live in a week.

Worth seeing what it would catch for you?
```
**Why it works:**
- The opening question exposes a gap the reader can't ignore (Jed: "the most important part of the email").
- It works across a whole segment without any personalization.

## 6. Creative ideas (Eric Nowoslawski style)

```
Subject: 3 ideas for kova

Hi Lena,

Had a look at Kova's site and put together 3 ideas to get more from your existing traffic:

1. Add a quote calculator to the pricing page
2. Text back missed calls within 60 seconds
3. Send a review request after each install

Happy to send a short breakdown of any of them. Which one's most useful?
```
**Why it works:**
- It gives value before asking (reciprocity).
- The ideas are specific to their business, which is what Nowoslawski reports as his best-performing angle.
- The CTA is easy to answer with "#2".

## 7. Follow-up sequence (steps 2–4)

Under the paired-thread default (`../knowledge/09-sequences.md`), step 2 replies in thread 1, step 3 starts a new thread with a new subject, and step 4 replies in thread 3.

```
Hi Dana,

One more angle: ramping reps usually also means managers spend hours a week on call reviews.

Northwind cut that to about 20 minutes per rep. Want me to send you the breakdown?
===
Hi Dana,

If onboarding sits with someone else on your team, who should I ask?
===
Hi Dana,

Sounds like the timing's off, so this is my last note.

If SDR ramp time becomes a priority, just reply "ramp" and you'll get the breakdown.
```
**Why it works:**
- Every step adds something new: a new problem, then a referral ask, then real closure.
- Nothing says "just following up".
- The breakup uses loss aversion and offers a one-word reply.

# Part 2: From the creators (verbatim, curated)

The S-numbers match `agents/cold-email/research/web/04-copywriting-swipe-library.md`, which has the teardowns and source URLs. Results are self-reported.

### S1 · Josh Braun · 4T (2022)
```
James, noticed your SDRs target HR/Benefit Directors in companies > 500 employees.

Have you tried reverse engineering high-performing cold emails to boost response rates?

4k+ reps are seeing an uptick. It involves a guide with copywriting + underlying psychology of cold emails with proof of positive responses you can steal.

Here's a peek.
```
**Pattern:** fact → status-quo question → peer proof → free-taste CTA.

### S2 · Josh Braun · Before → After, Datarails (2024)
```
Kim,
You shouldn't have to manually map financials from QBO, put them in a spreadsheet, and change all of the formulas to complete financial close.

CFOs use Datarails to download reports in a day. No adjusting, mapping, calculating, or recalculating data. Use your Excel models without having to change anything.

Think this might help?
```
**Pattern:** the customer's own "before" words → a concrete "after".

### S3 · Josh Braun · Detached expert question, Pop Card (2023)
```
Hey James - I saw that Mario's has four positive Google reviews. Some customers don't leave a review because it takes too much time or because they don't know where/how to post it on the Internet.

Have you looked into Pop Card?
```
**Pattern:** public fact → a hidden problem → no needy CTA. [TEST]

### S7 · Will Allred · Safe Space rewrite (2021)
```
Hey Will,
Saw you're hiring for several different roles. Curious if I'm wrong to guess that a % of that will be to backfill roles.
We're seeing this across the board with recruiting leaders in Food and Bev.
They need to keep growing, but they're now also having to account for folks leaving.
We've been helping folks in your space find great talent.
Worth a chat to see how we can help your team?
```
**Pattern:** trigger → hedged hypothesis → "you're not alone" → restate the problem → solution → CTA.

### S8 · Will Allred · Mouse Trap (2022)
```
Will, looks like you're hiring reps.
Would it be helpful to get a more granular look at how they're ramping on email?
```
**Pattern:** a two-line trigger plus a value question. Ideal for email 2 or mobile.

### S15 · Eric Nowoslawski · GEX "make more money" (2024, in daily use)
```
Subject: cheers to the role

Hey {{first_name}} - LinkedIn told me you took over the VP of Sales role at {{company_name}}. I did some digging and saw you don't have an SDR team yet.
Do you have a plan to build pipeline with such a lean team?
In the past year, we've helped companies like Prospeo build over $3M in pipeline selling a $99 product with our outbound service that's half the cost of an SDR.
We always start with a free test to validate your offer works with cold email.
If we could help you generate pipeline for 2025, would it be useful to connect?

P.S. That free test is really free and we can get it launched in less than 15 minutes on a call together. Just to test out for you if cold email is a channel you should jump into.
```
**Variants:**
- *Save money:* line 2 becomes "Have you thought about outbound automation so you can save money from hiring a team of SDRs?"
- *Save time:* line 2 becomes "Do you have any outbound systems running yet or are you looking to get started?" with proof "Last week alone, we helped Boundless Macs, Donut, and Reputation Cure validate that they could get leads with cold email by launching a free campaign for them on one 30 minute call."

### Reply template · Josh Braun · "Not interested" (via Florin, about 50% respond)
```
Totally understand. I'm sure you probably get dozens of emails offering solutions to help with compensation. And I'm sure you delete most of them.
```

### Anti-examples (never do this)
- **S6** (Allred's "before"): the sender's life story plus a 15-minute ask.
- **S21** (Eric's "growth"): a saturated value prop that structure can't save.
- **S37** (Eric's "my friend needs this"): deceptive bait he vowed never to repeat.
- **Sam Nelson's "I don't want to be rude but…"**: a guilt breakup.
