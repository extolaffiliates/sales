# Cold Email Copywriting Methodologies: Credible Practitioners and 2025–2026 Data

Compiled 2026-10-22 for the "master cold email agent" project.

## Read this first: method and limits

- **How this was gathered:** WebSearch only. WebFetch failed with DNS errors (`getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND`) on every host tried (lavender.ai, substack, 11x.ai, hunter.io), and the container's direct HTTPS is blocked by the proxy (403). So no page, PDF, YouTube transcript or newsletter was opened in full. Everything below comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages.
- **What that means for verbatim quotes:** The search tool returns short quoted fragments, not whole emails. It would not reproduce full copyrighted templates. **No complete verbatim example email could be retrieved for any creator.** Where I quote, the text is a verbatim fragment as it appeared in the search extract (marked **[V]**). Everything else is a paraphrase (marked **[P]**). Section 12 lists the exact URLs that hold the full example emails, so a follow-up pass with a working fetcher can pull them.
- **Primary vs. secondary:** Each claim notes whether the source is the creator's own page (primary) or a third-party summary (secondary). Vendor benchmark numbers are self-reported and use different denominators. Published averages vary by as much as 7.6x depending on methodology ([lemlist](https://www.lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-response-rate)).
- **Nothing was invented.** Where something you asked about could not be found (for example a 30MPC "Problem-Proof-Ask" framework, Lavender "you vs I" data, or the name "Experts on Demand"), I say so.

## 1. Alex Berman (Cold Email Wizard / alexberman.com; co-author of *The Cold Email Manifesto*, 2018)

**Core philosophy**
- [P] A short, formulaic email built around one specific, relevant proof point. He says this beats clever copy. Primary source: [alexberman.com/cold-email-templates](https://alexberman.com/cold-email-templates).
- [P] Match the proof to the prospect. [V] "if you're targeting restaurants and your case study is a SaaS company, you've lost them. Match the proof to the prospect." ([alexberman.com/cold-email-templates](https://alexberman.com/cold-email-templates))

**Structure.** [P] Subject → one-line specific compliment → one-line case study → simple ask. His site describes it as: [V] "subject line 'Quick Question', one specific compliment, one case study with real numbers, and a simple ask" ([alexberman.com/cold-email-templates](https://alexberman.com/cold-email-templates)). A book summary has the same order: [V] "This case study will be the second line of your cold email template, with a custom compliment as your first line." ([rasulkireev.com summary of the Manifesto](https://rasulkireev.com/cold-email-manifesto/)).
- Case-study line formula = specific client + specific result + specific timeframe. His example: [V] "I specialize in finding new clients for web and app developers." He follows it with a named client and result (McDonald's, an extra $1M in six months, per the extract) ([alexberman.com/cold-email-templates](https://alexberman.com/cold-email-templates)). He adds: [V] "no flowery language, no hype, just specific client + specific result + specific timeframe."
- *Manifesto* example (fragment only) [P]: an iOS developer for healthcare cites a Johns Hopkins app that raised patient happiness 75%, then asks whether Baylor wants the same. The CTA offers to send times ([Goodreads quotes page](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/96316416); [rasulkireev.com](https://rasulkireev.com/cold-email-manifesto/)).

**Subject lines**
- Historically [P] "Quick question" was his default, and "Question about [Company]" was a top performer for his agency ([Single Grain interview](https://www.singlegrain.com/videos/how-to-write-cold-emails-that-always-get-read/)). He also lists "[Mutual connection] suggested I reach out" as the highest open rate ([alexberman.com](https://alexberman.com/cold-email-subject-lines)).
- **Updated view (2025–26, his own site):** [V] avoid phrases like "Quick question for you" or "Thought this might be useful" — "these have been overused enough that they now read as automation, not human outreach". He now favors specific, contextual subject lines ([alexberman.com/cold-email-subject-lines](https://alexberman.com/cold-email-subject-lines)). **This is an internal contradiction across his content. The newer guidance should win.**

**Opener rules.** [P] The first line is a personalized compliment about something specific to the recipient ([rasulkireev.com](https://rasulkireev.com/cold-email-manifesto/)).

**CTA rules.** [P] One single, low-friction yes/no ask. Example: [V] "Worth 15 min this week?" He contrasts it with vague "let me know if interested" lines (alexberman.com template pages; [cold-email-templates](https://alexberman.com/cold-email-templates)).

**Follow-up.** Not retrievable in detail. He has a re-engagement page ([alexberman.com/re-engagement-email](https://alexberman.com/re-engagement-email)), which was not read.

**Data points.** [P] He claims the "foundation" template produced $600K ARR in 60 days. A secondary source says 30 days. Both are self-reported and unverified ([alexberman.com](https://alexberman.com/cold-email-templates)).

**Not found:** "Experts on Demand" and the "3-line email" as named items. The compliment/case-study/ask structure is effectively a three-line body.

## 2. Eric Nowoslawski (Growth Engine X; Clay power user)

**Core philosophy**
- [P] "Send the same email you would send manually, but to your whole market." This is the Growth Engine X positioning ([Instantly expert page](https://instantly.ai/experts/eric-nowoslawski); [Salesforge review](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/growth-engine-x-review)).
- [P] The offer matters more than personalization. Every offer must answer "why wouldn't they respond?" and handle hidden objections upfront ([Salesforge review, 2026](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/growth-engine-x-review-a-data-driven-cold-email-agency-2026), secondary).
- [P] Relevance first. AI personalization only amplifies an offer that already resonates. His 7-tips post calls AI personalization [V] "a multiplier, not a miracle worker" ([LinkedIn, ~2023](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/outboundphd_7-cold-email-tips-from-a-guy-that-sends-over-activity-7128010776023306242-OR6H)).
- [P] Put a real opinion or insight in the email, earned from customers or industry work, not from blogs. [V] "share your opinion on what people could be doing better or are doing wrong today with their current solution" (same LinkedIn post).
- [P] Treat cold email like an ad network. Run many simultaneous campaigns ("20–50"), test angles, offers, CTAs, case studies and subject lines quickly ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/outboundphd_in-2-years-cold-email-is-going-to-look-more-activity-7025864305149366272-Otwz); [thesignal.club](https://www.thesignal.club/p/inside-eric-nowoslawskis-ai-powered-cold-outbound-machine)).

**Structure and signals**
- **"Creative ideas" campaign** [P]: reported as Growth Engine X's best-performing angle. AI generates three specific ways the product could help each prospect's business ([Salesforge review](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/growth-engine-x-review), secondary). His Clay Marketplace "Campaign Angle Finder" fills three sender-defined bullet slots with company-specific, evidence-backed details ([marketplace.clay.com/eric-nowoslawski](https://marketplace.clay.com/eric-nowoslawski)).
- [P] Other Clay skills he publishes: a funding-signal line built only from verified recent raises, and a "specificity rewrite" that anchors the email to something the company actually sells ([Clay marketplace](https://marketplace.clay.com/eric-nowoslawski)).
- [P] AI writes only the first line (trigger or recent achievement); the rest is a human-written template. This is partly to reduce spam risk ([antoinebuteau.com](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-eric-nowoslawski/); [truevibess substack](https://truevibess.substack.com/p/how-we-automated-eric-nowoslawskis), secondary).

**Length.** [P] Under 75 words, aim for a response not a sale, one clear CTA ([Clay blog](https://www.clay.com/blog/b2b-cold-email-copywriting); attribution to Eric is via Clay's write-ups).

**Follow-up**
- [P] The first email gets the most positive replies, email 2 "did ok", and emails 3–5 [V] "really dropped off the deep end". Hence shorter sequences ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/outboundphd_the-more-cold-emails-i-send-the-more-i-feel-activity-7116225411360935936-puth)).
- [P] For large lists (50k+): send 1–2 emails within a week, then pause at least 45 days ([Clay blog](https://www.clay.com/blog/b2b-cold-email-templates)).
- [P] The breakup email that asks who else to speak to outperforms other follow-ups (same Clay post).

**Metrics.** [P] Track positive reply rate, and the ratio of trigger present to positive replies, to see which signals pay off ([Clay blog](https://www.clay.com/blog/b2b-cold-email-templates)).

**Data.** [P] Volume is reported at 1–1.5M emails a month ([Smartlead case study](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/case-study-eric-nowoslawski-growth-engine-x)). No verifiable reply-rate figures.

**Not found:** a verbatim full example. His public Google doc ("everything I know about cold email") is linked from [this LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/outboundphd_i-put-everything-i-know-about-cold-email-activity-7163534389954486274-gCRj).

## 3. Josh Braun (Poke the Bear; joshbraun.com)

**Core philosophy**
- [P] Ditch the pitch. Ask neutral, non-leading questions that get prospects to rethink their status quo. Leading questions make prospects defensive ([joshbraun.com/ditch-the-pitch-poke-the-bear](https://joshbraun.com/ditch-the-pitch-poke-the-bear/)).
- [P] Detachment: outreach is "sorting, not selling". Drop the assumption that they need you. He says this lowers the prospect's feeling of being manipulated ([joshbraun.com](https://joshbraun.com/how-to-cold-call-without-sounding-salesy/)).
- [P] Curiosity comes from "meaningful difference". Raise something the prospect doesn't already know rather than restating what they know ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_cold-email-tip-would-this-pique-your-curiosity-activity-7158170556452786176-8rc3)).
- [P] Wording should lower the "Zone of Resistance" so the reader doesn't feel criticized ([joshbraun.com/killer-cold-email-that-landed](https://joshbraun.com/killer-cold-email-that-landed/)).
- "Jedi mind tricks" as a named concept was **not found** in results.

**Structure: the 4T framework.** Truth (or Trigger) → Think → Third-party credibility → Talk ([LinkedIn 4T post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_lets-write-a-good-cold-email-that-sounds-activity-7172931819909169153-2rGj); [LeadIQ workshop](https://leadiq.com/blog/writing-a-great-cold-email-with-the-4t-template)).
- [V] "The first T stands for Truth - something factual that relates to a specific problem you solve." Another post frames it as a deduction: [V] "If A, then B is probably true." ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_heres-a-cold-email-copywriting-framework-activity-6963097038074880000-uqFf))
- Worked example [P]: the trigger is low YouTube views → a "think" question about reaching a bigger audience → a credibility line naming creators → a low-pressure offer ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_lets-write-a-good-cold-email-using-the-4-activity-6974715469949767680-AbdO)).
- [P] A "not sure if" hedged observation beats a direct "are you expanding your sales team?" question ("amateur vs pro" example in his 4T post).

**Opener rules.** [P] Generic compliments [V] "feel insincere" ([joshbraun.com/the-biggest-cold-email-mistake](https://joshbraun.com/the-biggest-cold-email-mistake/)). He said the email he featured [V] "would have still landed without the sugary first paragraph" ([killer-cold-email-that-landed](https://joshbraun.com/killer-cold-email-that-landed/)). He has first-line cheat sheets ([PDF](https://joshbraun.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Cold-Email-First-Stentence-Cheat-Sheet.pdf); [PDF](https://joshbraun.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/11-Killer-Cold-Email-First-Lines-1.pdf)).

**CTA rules.** [P] Gauge interest; don't ask for time.
- Verbatim closers: [V] "No rush, but is this worth a conversation?" ([4T LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_lets-write-a-good-cold-email-that-sounds-activity-7172931819909169153-2rGj)). [V] "Open to learning more?" and [V] "Worth a conversation?" ([15 Cold Email Copywriting Principles PDF, 2021](https://joshbraun.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/15coldemail-copywriting.pdf)).
- Possible [V], unconfirmed: "I'm not sure if this is a fit for you folks, but if you'd like to learn more, would you be open to carving out some time to explore?" ([joshbraun.com 35% response rate post](https://joshbraun.com/how-to-get-a-35-cold-email-response-rate/)). The search tool flagged that it may be paraphrased.

**Follow-up.** [P] He uses breakup-style "going cold" emails only after 4–5 unanswered follow-ups. They ask for feedback, not a meeting ([joshbraun.com/goingcold](https://joshbraun.com/goingcold/)).

**Data.** [P] The headline claim is a 35% response rate ([post title](https://joshbraun.com/how-to-get-a-35-cold-email-response-rate/)). In a podcast he says he measures whether openers get a neutral or positive reaction, targeting about 85% ([salesman.com](https://salesman.com/how-to-create-and-scale-a-cold-email-prospecting-strategy-sales-leadership-show/)).

## 4. Will Allred / Lavender

**Core philosophy**
- [P] The goal of a cold email is a reply (a conversation), not a conversion. Asking for the next step too early optimizes for the wrong funnel metric ([LinkedIn](https://za.linkedin.com/posts/williamallred_are-your-cold-email-ctas-feeling-stale-activity-6988841016749359104-CbDS); [Mixmax interview](https://www.mixmax.com/blog/interview-lavender-co-founder-will-allred)).
- [P] Every cold email needs a valid, prospect-specific reason to reach out. High-priority prospects shouldn't get fully automated messages ([Exit Five podcast, 2025](https://exitfive.com/podcast/cold-email-lessons-from-real-outbound-campaigns-in-2025/)).
- [P] Mobile first. Allred says a prospect is [V] "8x more likely they'll see it on this screen first" ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/williamallred_this-is-your-emails-first-impression-its-activity-7115673379076022272-kdmb)). Lavender says 80% of cold emails are opened on a phone ([Email Teardown #4](https://lavender.ai/blog/email-teardown-4)). Preview text matters because it shows in phone and smartwatch notifications ([cold-email-101](https://lavender.ai/blog/cold-email-101)).

**CTA rules**
- Interest-based CTA, in two flavors: [V] "Open to hearing more? Opposed to a quick call?" ([LinkedIn](https://za.linkedin.com/posts/williamallred_are-your-cold-email-ctas-feeling-stale-activity-6988841016749359104-CbDS)).
- [V] "in a conversation, open questions should come up, but for a cold email, closed questions are great for getting a response" (same thread).
- [P] Better still are "conversation-starting" questions, such as whether they use technology X, rather than "is this something you'd be interested in learning more about?" ([Mixmax](https://www.mixmax.com/blog/interview-lavender-co-founder-will-allred)).
- [P] Lavender's frameworks put the CTA last as a "call-to-conversation" ([lavender.ai/blog/sales-email-frameworks](https://lavender.ai/blog/sales-email-frameworks)).

**Subject line rules** ([lavender.ai/blog/cold-email-subject-line-tips](https://lavender.ai/blog/cold-email-subject-line-tips); the page is about 3.5 years old, so roughly 2023 data):
- [V] "One to three is best. Two is optimal." Moving from 2 to 4 words reduced replies 17.5%.
- [V] "the subject line is not the place for questions" (−56% opens). Punctuation: −36% opens.
- [P] Title case is recommended; not using it is said to reduce opens 30%.

**Body data** (Lavender, self-reported; dates mostly unstated):
- [V] "0-50 word count emails receive 23% more replies"; [V] "3rd - 7th Grade clarity emails receive 1.66x more replies"; mobile-optimized emails get 24% more replies ([Lavender best practices](https://www.lavender.ai/blog/6879935-lavender-best-practices)).
- [P] A 3rd–5th grade reading level gets 67% more replies ([cold-email-101](https://lavender.ai/blog/cold-email-101)).
- [P] Personalized vs. template: a 50% → 250% increase in replies ([personalization process](https://lavender.ai/blog/how-to-build-a-cold-email-personalization-process)).
- 2024 data via a secondary source [P]: 25–50 words is best; more than one question hurts; zero questions +11%; an "informative" tone −26% ([gaidme](https://gaidme.com/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks-lavender)).
- **First Lavender Cold Email Benchmark Report, Feb 2026:** N = 231,818 emails across ~50k inboxes ([Allred on X](https://x.com/WillAllred117/status/2019290225651200308); [report](https://www.lavender.ai/blog/the-cold-email-benchmark-report)). It covers reply rates by department, seniority and industry, plus later "Content Insights". The secondary extract says "A-graded" emails lifted reply rates from 3.4% to an unknown figure (truncated) ([Woodpecker review](https://woodpecker.co/blog/lavender-ai/)).
- **"You vs I" ratio data: not found** in any source.

## 5. 30 Minutes to President's Club (Armand Farrokh, Nick Cegelski) + Gong + Jason Bay: the 85M+ email study (2025)

**"Problem-Proof-Ask": not found** under that name on any 30MPC source. The closest documented 30MPC structures:
- [P] First-email rules: a very short subject line; pair a tailored observation with the problem you solve; tie their problem to something you fix; no hard sell ([30MPC LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/30-minutes-to-president-s-club_sales-prospecting-activity-6960276272249802752-Ly3u)).
- [P] Episode #77 Playbook: one pain point per email; a "3x3" of specific problem → what you do → simple ask; no news hooks or formalities ([Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/episode/6w3OyZVjxHCtLnG9l8JfMN)).
- [P] Observation-led research: one company observation to form a hypothesis about priorities, plus one personal observation (Brian LaManna on [30MPC newsletter](https://www.30mpc.com/newsletter/the-8-discovery-questions-that-got-me-to-presidents-club)).
- [P] Sequence: about 10–14 touches over 30 days, switching the subject and problem to a second pain, then a breakup ([30MPC LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/30-minutes-to-president-s-club_prospecting-email-activity-7036379494890295296-CIFX)).

**85M+ cold email study with Gong (Jason Bay helped build the playbook), aimed at "what books meetings in 2025"** ([report PDF](https://tactics.30mpc.com/hubfs/The%20Ultimate%20Cold%20Email%20Data%20Report-1.pdf); [Gong guide](https://www.gong.io/resources/guides/how-to-master-cold-email-get-the-data-backed-guide-based-on-85-million-emails); [Outbound Squad ep. 393](https://www.outboundsquad.com/podcast/jason-bay-393)):
- Length: under 100 words. [V] "you really only get ~3-4 sentences", because most prospects open on their phone ([30MPC formula article](https://www.30mpc.com/newsletter/the-data-backed-cold-email-formula-the-exact-words-length)).
- Subject lines: 1–4 words is best and lowercase is favored ([30MPC subject lines](https://www.30mpc.com/newsletter/4-data-backed-subject-lines-to-get-your-cold-emails-opened); Gong report). An empty subject line gave +30% opens but −12% replies.
- Pitching reduces reply rates [V] "by up to 57%" ([Gong blog](https://www.gong.io/blog/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says); 30MPC).
- Sequence: [V] "the sweet spot is around 6-7 emails total" ([30MPC](https://www.30mpc.com/newsletter/the-data-backed-blueprint-for-multi-touch-prospecting)).
- Top 10% of reps book 8x more meetings (Gong; another Gong post says 8.1x).
- Secondary, unverified: emails with the word "AI" had a 36% lower reply rate. Activity-based signals scored about 4x higher than personal details on Gong's direct-reply index ([nodesparks](https://www.nodesparks.com/blog/cold-email-personalization-2026)). Gong's exec article says avoiding "AI" and "platform" helps, but gives no figure ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/do-execs-really-reply-to-cold-email-here-s-what-the-data-says)).
- Caveat: Gong posts cite 25M, 28M and 85M datasets in different places.

**Gong CTA data (older Gong Labs, roughly 2020, ~304K emails)** [P]: in cold emails, an interest CTA had a 30% success rate vs. specific-time 15% vs. open-ended 13%. Success meant a meeting booked within 10 days. **In-pipeline the result reverses:** specific date/time 37% ([Gong blog via growleads](https://growleads.io/blog/interest-based-ctas-vs-meeting-requests-study/); [Gong 7 laws PDF](https://www.gong.io/files/gong-guide-7-laws-of-highly-effective-sales-emails.pdf)). **Conflict:** a secondary report says Jason Bay's reading of the 85M data found offer-based CTAs beat interest-based CTAs 4x on replies (same growleads page). This is unverified.

**Jason Bay (Outbound Squad)**
- REPLY Method = Results, Empathy, Personalization, Laser-focus, You. Under 120 words / 3–5 sentences ([LinkedIn Pulse](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/reply-method-guide-increase-response-rates-setting-more-jason-bay)).
- 2025 PipeGen workshop [P]: 75-word ceiling, subject under 5 words, heavy personalization, an easy-to-answer CTA ([Outbound Squad](https://go.outboundsquad.com/pipegen-live-cold-email-a-repeatable-framework-for-double-digit-reply-rates-backed-by-science)).
- CTAs [P]: interest-based first, specific asks sparingly ("presumptuous"), avoid open-ended ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jasondbay_sales-prospecting-outbound-activity-7118566905648439296-DeRy)).
- [P] A "Hand Raiser" template asks the prospect to reply with a number ([starter pack](https://www.getmagical.com/templates/jbays-cold-email-starter-pack)). The June 2025 episode "The Offer" covers three offer types (not enumerated in the extract) ([ep. 387](https://www.outboundsquad.com/podcast/jason-bay-387)).

## 6. Jed Mahrle (Practical Prospecting newsletter)

**Core philosophy**
- [P] Messaging quality scales better than per-prospect research. Issue #147 (May 24, 2026), "Our #1 Cold Email Framework", is a three-part template that [V] "doesn't require signals, AI research, or any special data to run" ([content.practicalprospecting.io/p/147](https://content.practicalprospecting.io/p/147-our-1-cold-email-framework)).
- [P] Part 1 is a diagnostic opening question that exposes a capability gap: [V] "This is the most important part of the email." Parts 2–3 were not visible.
- [P] Signal-based personalization decays with scale: [V] "What produced a 4% reply rate on 500 contacts sometimes produces 1.5% on 5,000, because you've exhausted the warmest slice of your TAM." (#147)
- [P] Treat outbound as a properly A/B-tested experiment, not launch → tweak too early → abandon ([#79](https://jed.substack.com/p/79-why-cold-email-fails-and-what)).

**Tips from #148, "14 Cold Email Tips That Actually Work" (June 14, 2026)** ([link](https://content.practicalprospecting.io/p/148-14-cold-email-tips-that-actually-work)):
1. [P] Use the prospect's first name (or first name + company) as the subject line.
2. [P] Personalize with the prospect's own description of their niche (prompt-extracted). The more specific, the more personal.
3. [P] Put a real product screenshot in email #2. His best-performing second emails include an image.
4. [P] Give the offer details; a name alone isn't enough.
- [P] Lines like [V] "I know you get a lot of emails like this" increase reply rates because they disarm the reader (June 2026 issue).

**Sequence design.** [P] Two email types: a first "value" email built around a signal, and later "context" emails that hold back extra information. Don't say too much at once ([#86](https://jed.substack.com/p/72-two-emails-every-sequence-needs)). One pain point per email; no fancy CTAs. A two-sentence "reply if interested" email can work when the offer is strong.

**Data.** Template pack: [P] "consistently yielding reply rates surpassing the 5% mark" ([getmagical](https://www.getmagical.com/templates/jed-mahrles-top-7-email-templates)). Older newsletter: a 1–3% booking rate is "the sweet spot". 30% LinkedIn reply rate across 25 clients in 2026 (a different channel). A Smartlead case study claims a 21% reply rate (vendor promo) ([Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/case-study-jed-mahrle)).

## 7. Becc Holland (Flip the Script)

**Core philosophy**
- [P] Personalization at scale, built on relevance. Her definitions: personalization is 1:1 messaging that can't be sent to anyone else; relevance is 1:many messaging for a cohort; they work best together ([Flip the Script webinar](https://www.flipthescript.com/personalization-to-relevance-webinar)). Her personalization is psychographic (how prospects think and behave), not demographic.
- [P] The "premise" is the reason for outreach, drawn from what the person or company has said or done.

**Structure.** [P] 3 lines (no more than 4): premise (line 1) → body that bridges the premise to your value prop → one CTA. The CTA asks for ONE time to unpack the content mapped in the premise ([PersistIQ](https://www.persistiq.com/how-to-personalize-at-scale/); [Chorus.ai deck PDF](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/5192515/Customer%20Summit%20Decks/Personalization%20At%20Scale%20-%20Becc%20Holland,%20Chorus.pdf)).

**Premise buckets.** [P] Sources disagree on 5 vs. 6. Self-authored content (what they wrote or said) ranks highest. The "Junk Drawer" (hobbies, schools) is the last resort. The Chorus deck lists "The 5 Premise Buckets", including Junk Drawer and "Postbound" ([deck](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/5192515/Customer%20Summit%20Decks/Personalization%20At%20Scale%20-%20Becc%20Holland,%20Chorus.pdf); [antoinebuteau.com](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-becc-holland/)).

**Follow-up.** [P] A 16-step multichannel sequence with 5 emails; "Email #5 is the highest conversion overall" ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/beccholland-flipthescript_for-most-of-my-prospecting-sequences-here-activity-6938519894162378753-fn-W)). A commonly attributed version is a 21-day cadence whose first follow-up adds value (relevant content) ([b2bhero](https://b2bhero.co/2022/02/08/the-best-cold-email-follow-up-sequence-after-no-response/), secondary). The material is mostly 2019–2022.

## 8. Sam McKenna (#samsales; "Show Me You Know Me", SMYKM)

## 11. Cross-creator synthesis

### A. Consensus rules (3+ independent sources agree)
1. **Short.** Under ~75–100 words, roughly 3–5 sentences. Sources: Gong/30MPC <100; Instantly elite <80; Eric <75; Jason Bay 75–120; Lavender 0–50 best and 25–50 in 2024 data; Hunter 20–39; Becc 3–4 lines; Berman 3 body lines. *The outlier is Belkins (101–200 best), and that figure is secondary and unverified.*
2. **Written for mobile.** Plain text, skimmable, digestible without scrolling (Lavender 80% opened on phone; 30MPC; Allred).
3. **Simple language.** Roughly a 3rd–7th grade reading level (Lavender). No buzzwords, ROI fluff, "AI" or "platform" (Gong).
4. **One email = one problem/idea and one CTA** (30MPC, Jed, Eric, Instantly, Jason Bay, Berman).
5. **Problem-first, not pitch-first.** Pitching −57% (Gong); "problem-first positioning" (Instantly); Braun's Truth/Think; 30MPC observation→problem; Jed's diagnostic question.
6. **A relevant, specific reason for reaching out** (observation, trigger or premise) beats generic flattery (Braun, Becc, McKenna, 30MPC, Eric, Allred). Company or activity signals appear to beat personal trivia (Becc ranks the "Junk Drawer" last; Gong's activity-signal finding, secondary).
7. **Specific proof with numbers, matched to the prospect's segment** (Berman's named client + result + timeframe; Braun's third-party credibility; 30MPC proof).
8. **Low-friction, closed, interest-based CTA for the first touch.** "Worth a conversation?", "Open to hearing more?", "Opposed to…?" (Braun, Allred, Jason Bay, Gong cold-stage data). Save specific time asks for in-pipeline (Gong).
9. **Short subject lines.** 1–4 words, no salesy language, no question marks per Lavender (Gong/30MPC, Lavender, Jason Bay <5, Jed's first-name subject).
10. **Follow-ups matter but decay.** About 40–60% of replies come after step 1 (Instantly 42%; Belkins 41.4% first-email share implies ~59% after). Reply rate per step falls steadily (Smartlead). A practical range is 3–7 emails.
11. **The offer is the biggest lever** (Eric, Hormozi, Jed's "give offer details", Jason Bay's "The Offer").
12. **Test like a scientist.** Isolate variables and judge on positive replies and meetings (Eric, Jed #79).

### B. Where they disagree
- **Personalization depth.** McKenna (deep, person-level SMYKM) and Becc (premise 1:1) vs. Jed #147 (a no-signal, message-quality framework scales better) and Eric (offer and relevance over personalization; AI only for line 1). Resolve by tier: deep 1:1 for top accounts, relevance by cohort for volume (Becc's own personalization + relevance model; Allred's "don't fully automate high-priority").
- **Compliment openers.** Berman builds the email on a line-1 compliment. Braun says generic compliments feel insincere and the "sugary" first paragraph was unnecessary. Synthesis: only a *specific, verifiable* observation, never generic praise.
- **Subject lines.** "Quick question" (old Berman) vs. Berman's own 2025–26 page calling it automation-sounding. First name only (Jed) vs. hyper-specific personal hooks (McKenna) vs. lowercase 1–4 words (Gong) vs. title case (Lavender, older). Questions in subjects: Lavender says −56% opens, while secondary Belkins data says question subjects open best. Opens are an unreliable metric since 2025 anyway.
- **Interest CTA vs. offer CTA vs. specific time.** Gong's cold-stage data favors interest CTAs 2x. A secondary report of Jason Bay's 85M analysis says offer-based CTAs reply 4x better. Berman uses "Worth 15 min this week?" (a time ask). Unresolved. Make it an A/B variable.
- **Sequence length.** Eric: short (1–2 emails for big lists, then rest 45 days). Gong/30MPC: 6–7. Instantly: 4–7. Becc: 5 emails in 16 steps, with the 5th converting best. 30MPC: 10–14 multichannel touches. Smartlead median: 3.
- **Questions in the body.** Braun and Jed lead with a question. Lavender's 2024 data (secondary) says zero questions +11% and more than one question hurts. Synthesis: at most one question, usually the CTA or the diagnostic opener.
- **Disarming meta lines.** Jed says "I know you get a lot of emails like this" *raises* replies (2026). Most others push for zero filler. Worth testing.
- **Product images.** Jed: screenshots in email #2 help. Most others imply plain text only (deliverability). Use only in follow-ups, and test.

### C. Dead or overused tactics (2025–2026)
- **"Quick question" subject and "Thought this might be useful"**: [V] "now read as automation, not human outreach" (Berman, [alexberman.com](https://alexberman.com/cold-email-subject-lines)).
- **Fake "Re:"/"Fwd:" subjects**: secondary sources say prospects recognize them and trust drops. A claimed Woodpecker 2026 figure is +15% opens but −62% replies (unverified; via [search compilation](https://www.mixmax.com/blog/cold-email-subject-lines-2026)). Also reported as a spam-filter and reputation risk.
- **Generic AI compliments / "sounds like AI"**: Braun says generic compliments feel insincere. Fully AI-written emails reportedly get 1–1.5% replies vs. a better result for an AI first line on a human template (LeadHaste internal data, secondary, [leadhaste](https://leadhaste.com/blog/ai-cold-email-writer-comparison-2026)). Eric uses AI for line 1 only. Something Inc. (Aug 2026) says the "AI tell" shows up in most senders' outreach in Lavender's 231K dataset ([somethinginc](https://somethinginc.com/blog/cold-email-sounds-like-ai-lavender-data/)).
- **Pitch-heavy feature dumps and ROI/buzzword claims** (Gong: pitching −57%; buzzwords/ROI reduce replies).
- **Empty subject lines**: more opens, fewer replies (−12%, Gong/30MPC).
- **Long emails (>200 words)** (Instantly 2.4x gap; Gong's sharp drop after 100 words).
- **Optimizing for open rate**: open tracking was unreliable through 2025 (Belkins; 30MPC notes opens may be spam filters).
- **"I hope this email finds you well"**: no 2025–26 data point found that measures it specifically. It falls under the consensus "no formalities/filler" rule (30MPC ep. #77: avoid formalities). Treat it as dead by consensus, not by measured data.
- **Scaling one signal-based campaign indefinitely**: warm segments exhaust (Jed: 4% → 1.5%).

### D. Implied default spec for the agent (derived from the above, not any one creator)
Subject: 1–4 plain words, contextual, no "Re:" and no "Quick question". Body: 40–90 words at grade ≤7. Line 1 is a specific observation or trigger (company or activity first, personal only if genuine). Line 2 states the problem or a diagnostic question in neutral, non-accusatory language. Line 3 is one quantified, segment-matched proof. Line 4 is one closed interest CTA. No "AI/platform/ROI" buzzwords, no feature list. Sequence: 3–5 emails over ~2–3 weeks, each adding a new angle (second problem, screenshot, breakup or referral ask). Large lists rest 45+ days between bursts. Measure positive reply rate and meetings per 1,000 contacts.
