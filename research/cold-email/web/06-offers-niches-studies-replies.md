## 1. Offer design for cold email

### 1.1 Hormozi's value equation and Grand Slam Offer
- **The value equation** [P]: Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice) ([supersummary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/summary/), [happycoders](https://www.happycoders.eu/books/100m-offers)).
- **Grand Slam Offer** [P]:
  - It bundles solutions, bonuses and risk-reversing guarantees into a package that is hard to compare on price.
  - It starts from a "starving crowd" with massive pain and the ability to pay.
  - Enhancers: scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees and names (same sources).
- **Guarantee types** [P], from reader notes on *$100M Offers*:
  - **Unconditional:** the strongest. It works like a trial: the buyer pays, then decides. It brings more buyers and some refunds.
  - **Conditional:** has terms attached.
  - **Anti-guarantee** and **implied/performance:** these also exist, but their definitions were not retrieved ([Goodreads notes](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/58612786-100m-offers/46948487-carlos-ramos/f6d80069-3dc2-46bb-a050-22218a804c92)).
- **The three lead magnet types in *$100M Leads*** [P], from secondary summaries:
  1. **Reveal a problem** they don't know they have.
  2. **Samples or trials.**
  3. **One step of a multi-step process.**

  Hormozi's definition is "a complete solution to a narrow problem that also reveals the next problem your paid offer solves". He also says the market judges your free material, so weak freebies damage trust ([glasp](https://glasp.co/discover/book/B0CFDR3TYV), [medium](https://medium.com/@loreviglietti/100m-leads-how-to-get-strangers-to-want-to-buy-your-stuff-by-alex-hormozi-75a733dbe8ce), [shortform ep. 969](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2025-11-18-episode-summary-generate-1000s-of-leads-in-any-niche-ep-969)).
- **Gap:** no source showed how agencies apply the value equation specifically to cold-email copy. The rules in §8 are our own synthesis.

### 1.2 Performance offers: pay-per-meeting and pay-per-lead
- **Price range:** published agency rates run $200–$700 per booked meeting. "With monthly retainers there is no guarantee of booked meetings; the agency only owes you effort" [P][VENDOR] ([Smartlead pricing guide](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/cold-email-agency-pricing)).
- **Examples of the model:**
  - **C17 Lab:** pay only when a qualified decision maker *attends*, with a stated 100–400 held meetings over 180 days. No independent reviews exist [P] ([Salesforge review](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/c17-review)).
  - **Forge Partnerships:** a KPI guarantee. "If they don't hit it, they'll run the campaign for free until they do" [P] ([qwilr](https://forgestrategy.qwilr.com/Learn-More-About-Forge-Partnerships-MtK6UaRQx66x)).
  - **cold.email:** the first 20 leads are handled at no risk, and no payment is due if the client doesn't continue [P] ([cold.email](https://cold.email/pay-per-lead)).
  - **Leadbird (Nick Abraham):** "we work on performance, so our clients pay us per every lead we generate" [V]. The 800+ meetings/month and 21% reply-rate claims are company-supplied. Salesforge notes that "qualified lead" is never defined [SELF-REPORTED] ([Smartlead interview](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/vaibhav-namburi-and-nick-abraham-interview), [Smartlead case](https://www.smartlead.ai/case-study/case-study-leadbird-by-nick-abraham), [Salesforge review](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/leadbird-review)).
  - **Cold Email Hackers:** pay-per-meeting, no retainer or setup fee, month-to-month. Case study: Treasure Financial went from 0 to 30+ meetings in under 30 days [SELF-REPORTED] ([Salesforge review](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/cold-email-hackers-review)).
- **Buyer-side lesson for our own offers:** buyers distrust performance offers that don't define what counts as a result. **Always define the result** ("a meeting held with a decision maker at a company of 10+ staff") inside the offer.

### 1.3 "Free work first" offers: test campaigns, audits, Loom, ideas
- **Growth Engine X (Eric Nowoslawski):** a free test campaign "so both sides can test working together", for a limited number of qualified B2B buyers.
  - The intake form asks what a customer is worth to the prospect.
  - It claims the most successful customers saw leads "within the first three days" [P][SELF-REPORTED].
  - Some agency types are excluded: software development, web design, SEO, CRO and recruiting ([tally form](https://tally.so/r/mRvWxd), [Salesforge review](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/growth-engine-x-review), [podcast](https://mythos.one/me/thegtmengineer/2664cb)).
- **Loom / personalized video:**
  - **FirstSales:** puts the text baseline at about 4.1% replies vs 10–16% for personalized video. It notes the deliverability risk of raw video links and about 1 hour of recording per rep per day [P][VENDOR] ([firstsales](https://firstsales.io/blog/video-cold-email-loom)).
  - **Intercom:** reply rates up 19% *relative* after adopting Loom [VENDOR].
  - **Practitioner claims:** "15% reply rate consistently with a screen-share audit format, compared with the 5–8% I was getting with regular text emails" [V][SELF-REPORTED], and separately 4% → 22% with 90-second videos [SELF-REPORTED] ([RevGenius](https://community.revgenius.com/members/4df870b1-30f2-4606-8730-80dafdf9a94f), [ciela](https://ciela.ai/blogs/how-to-get-ai-agency-clients-with-personalized-video)).
  - **Format that recurs:** a screen-share of the prospect's own site, naming 2–3 specific issues, then a CTA. Keep it under 4 minutes and use an embedded thumbnail, not a bare link [P].
  - **Saraev's early model (secondhand):** scrape 100 businesses in one niche, record a Loom showing how an automation would help each one, then cold email them [P] ([Goodreads repost](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25254404-he-s-earning-100-000-per-month-selling-automations)).
- **QuickMail's free-audit template:** names a few specific website issues, then: "I'd love to run through my learnings and suggested actions on a call with you." [V]. No reply rate is published ([quickmail](https://quickmail.com/sales-email-template)).
- **Alex Berman, as cited secondhand:**
  - Cold email problems usually trace back to the offer, not the copy.
  - Work backward from past results to "I help (niche) achieve (result)".
  - A niche, concrete offer beats "we do web design" [P] ([echai](https://echai.ventures/startingup/from/alex-berman), [connectsafely](https://connectsafely.ai/free/cold-email-templates/agencies-freelancers)).
- **Case study, one vendor source:** 150 e-commerce brands → 34% reply rate (51 replies) → 31 booked calls → 8 paying clients, using a video-personalization offer. About 60% of video recipients booked [SELF-REPORTED] ([prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/ai-cold-email-campaigns)).

### 1.4 Productized offers
- **Productized AI personalization:** a weekly outreach pack of AI-researched openers at $300–$1,500/month. This is a blog suggestion, not market data [P] ([AI Cheatcode](https://aicheatcode.substack.com/p/ai-cheatcode-profit-pulse)).
- **Competition from software:** AI cold email tools (Outboundy, ColdForge) pitch the same outcome, so a service offer must "differentiate on results rather than tooling" [P].
- **Gap:** no independent data on how much offer tests lift reply rates. That has to come from our own A/B tests.

### 2.2 Proven agency offers seen in the research
- **Database reactivation:** the agency imports a client's dormant leads and runs AI SMS/email to re-engage them and book meetings. It is called "the easiest AI offer to sell" because it needs no ad spend.
  - **Pricing ladder:** pure pay-per-appointment → setup fee + per-appointment → setup fee + revenue share.
  - **Vendor claims:** 4.4% average booked-appointment rate, peaking at 8.9% (Leads Now AI). 5–15% of a dormant list recovered (Ciela). "37%+ response" with no method shown (Scale Growth Partners) [VENDOR][SELF-REPORTED].
  - **Compliance:** SMS needs TCPA consent checks, DNC scrubbing and A2P 10DLC registration ([ciela](https://ciela.ai/blogs/database-reactivation-ai-offer-for-agencies), [leadsnow](https://leadsnow.ai/database-reactivation-services-usa/), [scalegrowthpartners](https://scalegrowthpartners.com/ai-database-reactivation/), [zenintech](https://zenintech.com/ai-automation/database-reactivation/)).
- **AI receptionist / missed-call text-back (home services, dental):**
  - **The "missed call test" for prospecting:** call local businesses after hours, note who doesn't answer, then email them a personalized note with a short demo recording [P] ([agenthustles](https://www.agenthustles.com/h/voice-ai-receptionist-for-local-businesses/)).
  - **Product pricing in the market:** $0–$299/month.
- **Liam Ottley's 2023 beginner process** [P] ([X post](https://x.com/liamottley_/status/1701195864583393330)):
  1. Generate niche ideas with ChatGPT.
  2. Score them against his criteria.
  3. Build the offer with Hormozi's method.
  4. Pick the highest-ROI deliverable.
  5. Build a list.
  6. Run outreach on Instantly plus Dripify (LinkedIn), **offering to set up the client's "system" for free**.
  7. Before that: 2–5 domains, about 3 weeks of warmup.
  8. "If there's no response after significant volume, change the offer or the copy."
- **Selling the end result, not the tool:** a roofer wants income, not videos (attributed to Ottley via a secondary blog) [P].
- **Maker School (Saraev), as marketing claims:** a 90-day first-paying-client guarantee. $184/mo, 2,100+ members. Saraev reduced his personal involvement in Nov 2025 [P][SELF-REPORTED] ([marksinsights](https://marksinsights.com/maker-school/)).
- **Not found:** Iman Gadzhi's or Jordan Platten's specific templates. Saraev's "Europe gets 4–5x more replies" claim was **not found in any indexed source**, but see §3.3: European data does lean in that direction.

## 3. Benchmarks by industry, seniority, size, region and timing

> Denominators differ: per send, per contact, all replies or positive only. Compare within one source only. Published averages vary up to 7.6x by method ([lemlist](https://www.lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks)).

### 3.1 By industry (vendor blogs, 2026)
- **Healthcare:** 4–6% (strong campaigns 9–11%) in one source vs 2–4% in another.
- **Recruitment services:** 6–8%.
- **Real estate:** 3–6%, depending on whether you target agents, investors or owners; one table says 3.8%.
- **SaaS:** 3–5%, "often under 2%", or 4.1%.
- **Agencies/consultants:** 2–4% vs 4–7%.
- **Belkins:** Food & Beverage tops industries at 3.47% (per-send method, via Something Inc.) ([mailshake](https://mailshake.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks-2026/), [cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/cold-email-benchmarks-by-industry), [underfive](https://underfive.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rates-by-industry-2026-benchmarks/), [puzzleinbox](https://puzzleinbox.com/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks/), [somethinginc](https://somethinginc.com/blog/cold-email-response-rates-2026-data/)).

### 3.2 By seniority and company size
- **Belkins (7.5M emails, 2025):**
  - Founders and owners are the highest tier at 0.57%. C-level 0.42%. VPs lowest at 0.32%.
  - Company size: 0–10 employees 0.72% vs 10,000+ employees 0.22% [P].
- **Sales.co (about 1M emails):**
  - Total replies are almost identical for C-level (1.61%) and directors (1.68%).
  - **But C-level positive replies run at 0.270% vs 0.088% for directors, about 3x.** "The qualifier is company size" [P] ([sales.co CEO study](https://sales.co/research/should-you-cold-email-the-ceo), [sales.co stats](https://sales.co/research/cold-email-statistics)).
- **Conflicting small datasets:**
  - Snov.io: C-level 4.2% vs others 5.6%.
  - A consultant sample of 2,847 emails: C-suite 4.2%, directors 17.8%, managers 12.9% [SELF-REPORTED] ([warmer.ai](https://warmer.ai/blog/consultant-cold-email-response-rates-study-2025)).
- **Takeaway for the agent:** at small companies, email the founder or CEO. At large companies, go to the director or VP who owns the problem. Judge results on *positive* replies, not total replies.

### 3.3 By region
- **Belkins:**
  - "reply rates ranged from 1.43% in Poland down to 0.51% in the United States".
  - "European markets dominate the top 10".
  - The US is at 0.51% and the UK at 0.48%, despite having the largest send volumes [P]. The Poland and US figures could not be re-confirmed in a second search.
- **newlead.io (only 24 campaigns):** Sweden 6.10% vs US/Canada 0.95% ([newlead](https://newlead.io/research/cold-email-benchmarks-2026)).
- **B2B Rocket (2023):** Ireland, Slovenia and Denmark are highest; Azerbaijan, Japan and South Korea lowest.
- **Contradicting data:** Saleshandy says North America generated the highest reply rates, ahead of Western Europe and the UK ([saleshandy](https://www.saleshandy.com/blog/cold-email-statistics/)).
- **Formality and saturation:** "We do see increased formality in the UK & EU for executives" [V]. The US and UK are "one of the most saturated cold email markets" ([Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-outreach-us-uk/)).
- **Germany:** AiSDR found long, formal templates performed about as well as personalized emails there ([aisdr](https://aisdr.com/blog/cold-email-personalization-vs-templates.md)).
- **India:** no reliable email benchmark found. A 10–12% figure appears to be LinkedIn data.
- **Verdict on Saraev's "Europe 4–5x" claim:** the direction is supported by Belkins (Poland ≈ 2.8x the US) and newlead (Sweden ≈ 6x, on a tiny sample). The exact multiplier is unverified, and Saleshandy disagrees. **Treat it as a hypothesis to test**, and remember EU legal friction (§7).

### 3.4 Day and time
- **Belkins 2026:** Wednesday and Thursday 0.48% vs Monday and Tuesday 0.45%. The 8am–noon window is best at 0.54%. No single day clearly converts replies into meetings [P] ([belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/email-deliverability/choosing-the-best-time-to-send-an-email)).
- **CIENCE (6M emails):** 8am in the recipient's time zone is strongest; Wednesday has the most replies.
- **WarmySender (75K emails, via Qualtir):** Tuesday 9–11am, 4.8% [P].
- **Rule:** send Tuesday–Thursday, 8–11am in the recipient's time zone. The gaps are only tenths of a percentage point, so this is low priority.

### 3.5 Hooks, size and follow-ups
- **The Digital Bloom 2025:** "timeline hooks outperform problem-based hooks by 2.3× in reply rates and 3.4× in meetings booked". That is 10.01% vs 4.39% across 4 hook types (problem, social proof, numbers, timeline), 4 roles and 4 industries. Sample size not shown; no independent replication [V] ([digitalbloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/)). **Worth A/B testing.** A timeline hook is a time-bound result, for example "book 10 calls in your first 30 days".
- **Campaign size:** campaigns with 50 or fewer recipients get about 3x the replies of mass blasts (Hunter). Under 50 recipients reaches 5.8%+ vs 2.1% for over 1,000 (Prospeo) ([hunter](https://hunter.io/blog/cold-email-2022-vs-2025)).
- **Follow-ups:**
  - With follow-ups 4.9% vs without 3% (Hunter 2025).
  - About 4.8 touches to a first reply and 7.4 to a meeting (vendor).
  - Follow-ups recover close to half of all replies (SmartReach).
- **Reply → meeting conversion:** planning assumption **40–55% of positive replies become meetings**. Ranges run from 25–70%, and one 6M-email dataset shows about 27% (1,728 of 6,472). One source says replying within an hour gives 70–80% conversion, falling to 30–40% after 24h [VENDOR]. Instantly cites 15–25% of positive replies booking via calendar links ([cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/cold-email-benchmarks-by-industry), [growsurely](https://growsurely.com/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks), [instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks/)).
- **Meetings per contact:** 1–3% of contacts touched is "strong" [P].

## 4. Academic and controlled studies

These are the strongest evidence in the whole knowledge base, because the conditions were randomized.

| Study | Design | Finding | What it means for cold email |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sahni, Wheeler & Chintagunta (2018)**, *Marketing Science* 37(2):236–258, DOI 10.1287/mksc.2017.1066 [ACADEMIC] | Randomized field experiments with 3 firms, millions of emails | **Recipient's name in the subject line: +20% opens, +31% sales leads, −17% unsubscribes.** Personalization works "even when the email adds no informative content" | `{first_name}` subject lines are research-backed (and match Jed Mahrle's practice) |
| **Munz, Jung & Alter (2020)**, *Marketing Science*, DOI 10.1287/mksc.2019.1220 [ACADEMIC] | DonorsChoose, 30,297 donors | Sharing a surname (even just its first letter) increased opens, clicks and donations | **Real similarity** (name, origin, shared identity) lifts response, so mention a genuine commonality when you have one |
| **Rogers & Lasky-Fink**, *Writing for Busy Readers* [ACADEMIC/book] | About 7,000 school board members; 127-word vs 49-word request | **Shorter email: 4.8% vs 2.7% response, nearly 2x.** Readers skipped the long one or missed the ask at the end. 29% vs 15% thought the task would take under 5 minutes | Short emails lower the *perceived cost* of replying. Put the ask where it can't be missed ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jessica-stillman/double-your-chance-of-getting-a-response-to-emails-by-making-them-shorter.html), [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/388988/the-long-and-the-short-of-it-harvard-study-finds.html)) |
| **Lasky-Fink & Rogers (2022)**, *PLOS One*, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0276072 [ACADEMIC] | N = 439,150, pre-registered | A consistent "envelope" (sender, subject style) raises engagement when people expect value, and lowers it when they don't | Keep the sender identity and follow-up thread consistent *only if* email 1 delivered value |
| **Grant & Gino (2010)**, "A Little Thanks Goes a Long Way" [ACADEMIC] | 57 students; one added sentence of thanks | **Helping rose from 32% to 66%** after "Thank you so much! I am really grateful!". Fundraiser calls rose 50%+ | Thank people genuinely when replying, especially referrers and "wrong person" replies ([Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/?p=133210)) |
| **Althoff, Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil & Jurafsky (2014)**, ICWSM, arXiv:1405.3282 [ACADEMIC] | 21,000+ Reddit Random Acts of Pizza requests | Success rises with a clear statement of **need**, **gratitude**, **evidentiality** (verifiable details), **generalized reciprocity** ("I'll pay it forward") and the asker's **status** | Be specific and checkable, show credibility, offer to give back |
| **Guéguen (2002)**, *Computers in Human Behavior* 18(1):11–15 [ACADEMIC] | Email foot-in-the-door; 50 students | Agreeing to a small first request raised compliance with a bigger request 30 minutes later | Supports **small interest-CTA first, meeting later** |
| **Beaman et al. (1983), Burger (1999)**: foot-in-the-door meta-analyses [ACADEMIC] | 120 experimental groups | Effect is real but small (probit ≈ 0.32); the conditions matter | Foot-in-the-door helps but won't rescue a bad offer |
| **Roghanizad & Bohns (2016/17)**, *JESP*, DOI 10.1016/j.jesp.2016.10.002 [ACADEMIC] | In-person vs email requests | People **overestimate** how persuasive their emails are. Press coverage reports in-person requests at 34x | Assume the reader is less persuaded than you think: add proof and lower the friction. Use multichannel for high-value accounts |
| **Joinson & Reips (2007)**, *Computers in Human Behavior* 23(3):1372–1383 [ACADEMIC] | 3 studies, survey panels | A personalized salutation increased sign-ups, **but only when the sender had high power/status** | Personalization works better when it comes from a credible sender. Put credibility in the signature and the proof |
| **Heerwegh (2005)**, *Public Opinion Quarterly* 69(4):588–598 [ACADEMIC] | Web survey invitations | A personal salutation raised response | Use "Hi {first_name}", not "Hi there" |
| **Trouteaud (2004)**, via an ACT brief [ACADEMIC, secondhand] | Survey invitation subject lines | A plain "Please help" plea outperformed offer-style subjects and question subjects | Humble, direct subjects can beat clever ones |
| **Oldroyd, McElheran & Elkington (2011)**, *HBR* 89(3) [ACADEMIC] | Test leads sent to 2,241 US firms | Contacting a lead within 1 hour made a qualifying conversation **about 7x** more likely than waiting one more hour, and **60x** more likely than waiting 24h+. 23% of firms never responded | **Reply to positive replies within the hour** |
| **Kooti et al. (2015)**, WWW, arXiv:1504.00704 [ACADEMIC] | 16 billion emails, 2M users | Under overload, people reply faster but to a *smaller fraction* of emails. Mobile replies are faster and shorter | Easy, low-effort replies win in busy inboxes (one-word CTAs) |
| **Gornall & Strebulaev (2025)**, *Management Science* [ACADEMIC] | 80,000 pitch emails to 28,000 VCs and angels | Female senders got about 9% more interested replies; Asian senders about 6% more | Sender identity changes response, so test sender names, legally and honestly |
| **Bernstein, Korteweg & Laws (2017)**, *Journal of Finance* 72:509–538 [ACADEMIC] | 17,000 AngelList emails | Experienced investors reacted to **team** information, not traction | For sophisticated buyers, *who* is behind the offer is proof in itself |
| **Milkman, Akinola & Chugh (2015)**, *J. Applied Psychology* 100(6) [ACADEMIC] | 6,500 professors | White-male names got more meeting grants **for next week**. There was no gap for **same-day** requests | Distant time asks invite discretionary rejection |
| **Butler & Broockman (2011)**, *AJPS* [ACADEMIC] | 4,859 legislators | Responses depended on the sender's name and shared partisanship | Perceived similarity and in-group signals matter |
| **Kong et al. (CSCW 2022)**, arXiv:2302.11156 [ACADEMIC] | Organizational newsletter field experiment | Messages are recognized only if they appear in the **subject line or top position**; the evidence on subject length is mixed | The subject and first line carry the message; nothing below gets read |
| **Utrecht thesis (GPT-4o)** [ACADEMIC, student] | Simulated phishing, personalization levels | AI-personalized emails beat generic ones. **Organization-level personalization beat individual-level on conversions** | Company-level relevance can beat personal trivia (consistent with Gong/30MPC) |

**Not found:** a randomized field experiment on executives that crosses subject-line length with personalization, or a peer-reviewed field test of LLM-written cold emails. Burger, Messian & Patel (2004) on incidental similarity was not retrieved, so it isn't cited.

## 6. Reply and objection handling

### 6.1 Reply taxonomy (Instantly Unibox defaults)
The eight default tabs are All emails, **Interested, Meeting booked, Meeting complete, Closed, Out of Office, Wrong person, Not interested**. The AI labels add **Referral** and **Objection** [P] ([Instantly help](https://help.instantly.ai/en/articles/6576561-how-to-manage-the-unibox-best-practices-on-how-to-reply-to-leads), [Instantly agency playbook](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-cold-email-reply-management-for-agencies/)).

### 6.2 Objection frequency (Gong, 300M+ cold calls)
- "The top 5 most common objections actually account for 74% of all objections" [V].
- Category split, secondhand: **dismissive 49.5%, situational 42.6%, existing solution 7.9%** ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/we-found-the-top-objections-across-300m-cold-calls-heres-how-to-handle-them-all/)).
- **Implication:** about half of all pushback is a reflexive brush-off. Lower the stakes rather than arguing.

### 6.3 Responses by objection (practitioner guidance)
- **"Not interested":**
  - Josh Braun says "I'm not interested" isn't really an objection. Admit the outreach was a big ask, then pivot to one small question, not a pitch.
  - A "cool-off" email that asks them to reply with a short phrase if they'd rather not be contacted either filters them out or clarifies their position [P] ([Braun LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_im-not-interested-isnt-an-objection-activity-7021192488505737217-1VAe), [Braun LinkedIn 2](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_josh-a-prospect-responded-to-my-cold-email-activity-7074138982707556352-T9Cm)).
- **"Send me info" / "send me an email":**
  - Braun says it usually means a polite no. Mirror the phrase back, then ask permission for a few questions so that what you send is relevant [P] ([joshbraun.com/send-me-an-email](https://joshbraun.com/send-me-an-email/)).
  - Others: send 2–3 lines and one filtering question, not a PDF [P] ([moderninbound](https://moderninbound.com/blog/how-to-handle-cold-email-replies-and-objections-2026)).
- **"Not right now":** half the time it means "not now", not "never". Ask one specific question, log a date, and come back with something new [P].
- **"Too expensive":** reframe around the cost of doing nothing, or ask when the budget resets. Apollo's template acknowledges the concern, then points to peer results [P] ([Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/templates/objection-handling-email-templates)).
- **"We already have an agency/someone":** acknowledge it briefly, then ask one question about a specific gap or narrow area where a second option could help. Instantly frames it as a buying signal [P] ([Instantly scripts](https://instantly.ai/blog/3-scripts-for-b2b-cold-call-objections/), [skipcall](https://skipcall.io/en/blog/marketing-agency-cold-call-objections)).
- **"How did you get my email?":**
  - Name the specific source, confirm your identity, offer removal by reply, and link to your privacy notice.
  - Under GDPR Art. 14/21, the right to object must be flagged at the latest in the first communication ([Hunter GDPR](https://hunter.io/blog/how-to-do-cold-emailing-after-the-gdpr/), [Cognism](https://help-legacy.cognism.com/hc/en-gb/articles/36836552873618)).
  - Our draft, not from a source: "Thanks for asking. I found your address on [source]. I'm [name] at [company]. If you'd rather not hear from us, reply 'remove' and I'll delete your details. Our privacy notice is at [link]."
- **Structural rule:** "nearly every bad objection template has the same structural flaw: two questions in the closing line" [P] ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/sales-objection-email-templates-that-convert/)). End with **one** question.
- **Mirror the tone:** match the prospect's formality and length. Never answer a casual one-liner with "Dear… Sincerely" [P] ([Lavender help](https://help.lavender.ai/en/articles/5315551-mirroring-tones-in-replies)).
- **FirstSales (vendor):** "high-intent outbound converts 30 to 45% of replies into meetings when the rep answers fast, acknowledges the objection, and reframes around the prospect's actual priority" [VENDOR].
- **Vague CTAs stall positive replies.** "Let me know if you're interested" stalls conversion even after a yes, so give a concrete next step [P] ([Danish Lead Co.](https://danishleadco.io/blog/why-your-cold-emails-arent-booking-meetings-and-how-to)).

### 6.4 Speed
- HBR (Oldroyd et al.): within 1 hour is about 7x better than 2 hours, and 60x better than 24h+ (§4).
- Answer every positive reply within the hour during business hours, with one clear next step.

## 7. Compliance edge cases

> Not legal advice. These are mostly secondary sources; check the statutes and a lawyer before relying on them.

- **Buying lists:**
  - **GDPR:** sources split. One says legitimate interest "almost never works" for a list bought from a third party with no relationship to the recipients. Others say B2B legitimate interest works *if you document the source, the lawful basis and transparency*.
  - **CAN-SPAM:** no opt-in needed for B2B, but you need honest headers and subjects, a physical address, and an opt-out honored within 10 business days. There is no B2B exception.
  - **Deliverability risk:** most bought lists see under 5% opens and hurt your reputation ([instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/b2b-email-list-compliance-gdpr-canspam), [convert](https://www.convert.com/blog/privacy/gdpr-cold-emails-means-outbound-strategy/), [cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/buy-email-lists)).
  - **Rule:** only use data with provable provenance. Verify it, and record the source per contact.
- **Scraping case law (US):**
  - **hiQ v. LinkedIn:** ended Dec 2022 with a $500K consent judgment. hiQ had to stop scraping and delete its data. The court found the **user-agreement anti-scraping terms enforceable as contract** (breach). The Ninth Circuit's view was that breaching ToS alone doesn't trigger CFAA "without authorization" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiQ_Labs_v._LinkedIn), [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/12/linkedin-v-hiq-landmark-data-scraping-suit-provides-guidance-to-data-scrapers-and-web-operators)).
  - **Meta v. Bright Data:** N.D. Cal., Judge Chen, 23 Jan 2024. **Logged-off scraping of public data did not breach Meta's terms**; a logged-off scraper isn't a "user" ([Farella](https://www.fbm.com/technology/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/), [Quinn Emanuel](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/news-events/client-alert-meta-v-bright-data-significant-decision-for-web-scraping-industry/)).
  - **Rule:** don't scrape behind a login or with accounts. Public, logged-off data carries lower contract risk, but GDPR still applies to EU people.
- **Germany (UWG §7):**
  - Prior express consent is required for advertising email, **including B2B**. Under BGH I ZR 218/07 (2009), even a single unsolicited ad email to a business can be unlawful.
  - The burden of proof is on the sender, and an opt-out doesn't replace consent.
  - The exception is existing customers under §7(3), with an opt-out.
  - Cold *calling* B2B may rely on presumed consent, but **not email** ([lexetius](https://lexetius.com/2009,2183), [IHK Bonn PDF](https://www.ihk-bonn.de/fileadmin/dokumente/Downloads/Recht_und_Steuern/Wettbewerbsrecht/WerbungTelefonBriefE-Mail.pdf), [overloop](https://overloop.com/blog/b2b-cold-email-germany-gdpr-compliance)).
  - **Rule: no cold email to German recipients without consent.**
- **Austria (TKG 2021 §174):**
  - Direct-marketing email needs prior consent. Fines run up to €50,000 (WKO).
  - A 2023 BVwG decision upheld a €500 fine against a managing director personally.
  - The exceptions for existing customers apply; any B2B carve-out is unconfirmed ([WKO](https://www.wko.at/service/wirtschaftsrecht-gewerberecht/E-Mails_versenden_-_aber_richtig.html), [RIS BVwG](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/Bvwg/BVWGT_20230816_W157_2262141_1_00/BVWGT_20230816_W157_2262141_1_00.pdf)).
  - **Treat Austria like Germany.**
- **India:**
  - There is no blanket anti-spam law for email. TRAI's TCCCPR covers telecom (calls and SMS).
  - **The DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025** were notified 13–14 Nov 2025. Full compliance is due by **13 May 2027**, with penalty tiers up to ₹250 crore. Sources conflict on whether penalties apply from Nov 2026 or May 2027.
  - **§3(c)(ii)** excludes personal data that the person themselves made public, or caused to be made public. Commentators call it narrower than "findable online"; data published by an employer or the media may not qualify. No ruling on scraping yet.
  - Sources: [Wikipedia DPDP Rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Rules,_2025), [india-briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/), [law.asia](https://law.asia/publicly-available-data-dpdpa/), [IAMAI to TRAI](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/IAMAI_18102024.pdf).
  - **Rule:** for Indian recipients, be transparent, honor opt-outs, use data the person published themselves, and plan for DPDP consent by 2027.

**Offer rules derived from this research:**
1. **Define the result** in the offer itself (what counts as a meeting or lead). Buyers distrust vague performance offers (§1.2).
2. **Name an outcome with a number and a timeframe**, not time saved. Timeline hooks may beat problem hooks (§3.5).
3. **Lower the first step:** use a free sample, an audit or an idea before asking for a meeting. Foot-in-the-door supports this (§4).
4. **Match the risk reversal to the buyer.** Use pay-per-result for skeptics and a guarantee for retainers.
5. **The freebie must be genuinely good.** Hormozi: the market judges your free work.
6. **Revenue per reply matters more than reply rate.** A lower reply rate to higher-value buyers can win (§5, Puzzleinbox).

## 9. Rules this research adds to the agent

1. **Use `{first_name}` in the subject line.** +20% opens and +31% leads in a randomized study (Sahni et al.). Make it the default A/B control.
2. **Shorter wins in field experiments too.** 49 words nearly doubled response vs 127 (Rogers & Lasky-Fink). Keep the ask easy to see.
3. **Reply to positive replies within 1 hour** (HBR: about 7x). Plan on 40–55% of positive replies becoming meetings.
4. **Thank people.** One sentence of gratitude doubled helping (Grant & Gino). Use it in referral and "wrong person" replies.
5. **Mention a genuine similarity** (shared name origin, background or community) when you have one. Never fabricate it (Munz et al.; Butler & Broockman).
6. **Credibility multiplies personalization** (Joinson & Reips). Strong signature, real proof.
7. **Pick the seniority by company size:** founder or CEO at under 50 staff, the problem owner (director or VP) in enterprise. Judge on positive replies (Sales.co).
8. **Region:** Europe *may* reply more (Belkins), but Germany and Austria need **consent** for cold email. Default to excluding DE/AT and test other EU countries with GDPR-compliant footers.
9. **Objections:** one question, no arguing, mirror their tone. "Send info" usually means no, so mirror and qualify (Braun).
10. **Offers:** lead with free work, a sample, or a pay-per-result offer with a **defined result**. Test timeline hooks.
